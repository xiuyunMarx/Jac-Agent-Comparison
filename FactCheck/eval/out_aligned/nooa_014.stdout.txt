Verdict: NOT_SUPPORTED
Rationale: The claim contains multiple contradicted material parts. First, the SportAccord World Mind Games were inaugurated in December 2011 in Beijing, not Melbourne, as stated in the SportAccord World Mind Games article: "The International Mind Sports Association (IMSA) inaugurated the SportAccord World Mind Games December 2011 in Beijing." Second, there is no evidence that Lubabalo Kondlo won a silver medal at the 2012 SportAccord World Mind Games. His Wikipedia article describes him only as a draughts grandmaster and former GAYP world champion, with no mention of the SportAccord World Mind Games at all. Third, the Sergio Scarpetta article reveals that it was Scarpetta—not Kondlo—who won the silver medal in the checkers competition at the SportAccord World Mind Games, and that was in December 2014 in Beijing, not in 2012. The claim conflates the 2012 World Mind Sports Games (held in Lille, France) with the SportAccord World Mind Games (held in Beijing), and incorrectly attributes both the silver medal and the inauguration city.
