Verdict: NOT_SUPPORTED
Rationale: The claim has two material parts: (1) that an 'Inglis Barracks bombing' was an IRA attack in England, and (2) that it was the first such attack since the Brighton Hotel bombing targeting Margaret Thatcher. The second part is supported: the Brighton Hotel bombing article confirms 'On 12 October 1984 the Provisional Irish Republican Army (IRA) attempted to assassinate members of the British government, including the prime minister, Margaret Thatcher, at the Grand Hotel in Brighton, England.' However, the first part is contradicted. The Wikipedia article on Inglis Barracks describes it only as 'a military installation in Mill Hill, London, NW7' that 'has been redeveloped and now contains a variety of modern housing,' with no mention of any bombing. Multiple targeted searches for 'Inglis Barracks bombing,' 'Mill Hill bombing IRA,' and variations across years 1988–1989 all returned no information about any bombing at that location. Other barracks bombings (Deal, Chelsea, Thiepval) are documented in the evidence, but no Inglis Barracks bombing appears anywhere. Per the decision rule, a Wikipedia article about an entity that does not mention a claimed attribute or event is evidence against that part. Since a material part of the claim is contradicted, the claim is NOT_SUPPORTED.
