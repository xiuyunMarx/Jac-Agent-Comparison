world\"))\n\nThis is a **list of oldest extant buildings**.\n\n## Criteria\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=List_of_oldest_extant_buildings&action=edit&section=1\n\"Edit section: Criteria\")]\n\nA [building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Building \"Building\") is defined as\nany human-made structure used or interface for supporting or sheltering any\nuse or continuous [occupancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupancy\n\"Occupancy\"). In order to qualify for this list, a structure must:\n\n  * be a recognisable building;\n  * incorporate features of building work from the claimed date to at least 1.5 metres (4.9 ft) in height;\n  * be largely complete or include building work to this height for most of its perimeter.\n  * contain an enclosed area with at least one entry point.\n\nThis deliberately excludes ruins of limited height and statues. The list also\nexcludes:\n\n  * [dolmens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolmen \"Dolmen\"), a type of single-chamber megalithic tomb, usually consisting of three or more upright stones supporting a large flat horizontal capstone. Dolmens were typically covered with earth or smaller stones to form a [tumulus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tumulus \"Tumulus\") (which are included in the list). In many instances, that covering has weathered away, leaving only the stone \"skeleton\" of the burial mound intact. [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic \"Neolithic\") dolmens are extremely numerous, with over 1,000 reported from [Mecklenburg-Vorpommern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megaliths_in_Mecklenburg-Vorpommern \"Megaliths in Mecklenburg-Vorpommern\") in Germany alone.[1]\n  * [cairns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cairn \"Cairn\"), which are simply large piles of loose stones (as opposed to [chambered cairns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chambered_cairn \"Chambered cairn\"))\n  * [standing stone rings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standing_stones \"Standing stones\"), such as [Stonehenge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stonehenge \"Stonehenge\"), also do not count because they are not enclosed and do not have roofs.\n\nDates for many of the oldest structures have been arrived at by [radiocarbon\ndating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiocarbon_dating \"Radiocarbon dating\")\nand should be considered approximate.\n\n## By age\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=List_of_oldest_extant_buildings&action=edit&section=2\n\"Edit section: By age\")]\n\nThe following are amongst the oldest buildings in the world that have\nmaintained the requirements to be such.
