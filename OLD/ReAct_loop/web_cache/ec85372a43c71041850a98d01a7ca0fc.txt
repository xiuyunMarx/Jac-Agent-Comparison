\nThe **Temple of Hercules Victor**\n([Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_language \"Italian language\"):\n_Tempio di Ercole Vincitore_) or **Hercules Olivarius**\n([Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_language \"Latin language\") for\n\"Hercules the [Olive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olive_Branch \"Olive\nBranch\")-Bearer\")[1] is a\n[Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Roman_religion \"Ancient Roman\nreligion\") [temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_temple \"Roman temple\")\nin [Piazza Bocca della\nVerit\u00e0](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piazza_Bocca_della_Verit\u00e0 \"Piazza Bocca\ndella Verit\u00e0\"), the former [Forum\nBoarium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forum_Boarium \"Forum Boarium\"), in\n[Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome \"Rome\"),\n[Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy \"Italy\"). It is a\n[tholos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tholos_\\(architecture\\) \"Tholos\n\\(architecture\\)\"), a round temple of Greek\n'[peripteral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peripteral \"Peripteral\")' design\ncompletely surrounded by a colonnade. This layout caused it to be mistaken for\na temple of [Vesta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vesta_\\(mythology\\) \"Vesta\n\\(mythology\\)\") until it was correctly identified by Napoleon's Prefect of\nRome, [Camille de Tournon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camille_de_Tournon\n\"Camille de Tournon\").[2]\n\nDespite (or perhaps due to) the Forum Boarium's role as the cattle market for\nancient Rome, the Temple of Hercules is the subject of a folk belief claiming\nthat neither flies nor dogs will enter the holy place.[3] The temple is the\nearliest surviving mostly intact marble building in Rome and the only\nsurviving one made of Greek marble.[4]\n\n## Description\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Temple_of_Hercules_Victor&action=edit&section=1\n\"Edit section: Description\")]\n\n[![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/97/Reconstruction_drawings_of_the_Temple_of_Hercules_Victor%2C_by_J._A._Coussin%2C_1802_%2801%29.webp/250px-\nReconstruction_drawings_of_the_Temple_of_Hercules_Victor%2C_by_J._A._Coussin%2C_1802_%2801%29.webp?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Reconstruction_drawings_of_the_Temple_of_Hercules_Victor,_by_J._A._Coussin,_1802_\\(01\\).webp)Jean\nAntoine Coussin, _The Temple of Vesta or Hercules Victor_ , 1802\n(reconstruction)\n\nIt is dated to the later 2nd century BC and was built either by [L. Mummius\nAchaicus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L._Mummius_Achaicus \"L. Mummius\nAchaicus\"), conqueror of the\n[Achaeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaean_League \"Achaean League\") and\ndestroyer of [Corinth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Corinth \"Ancient\nCorinth\"),[5] or by the [trader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_economy\n\"Roman economy\") [Marcus Octavius\nHerrenus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herennia_gens#Members \"Herennia gens\")\nin gratitude for success in business.[6] The temple is 14.8 m in diameter and\nconsists of a circular [cella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cella \"Cella\")\nwithin a concentric ring of twenty [Corinthian\ncolumns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corinthian_order \"Corinthian order\")\n10.66 m tall, resting on a [tuff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuff \"Tuff\")\nfoundation. These elements supported an\n[architrave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architrave \"Architrave\") and roof,\nwhich have disappeared.[6]\n\nThe original wall of the cella, built of\n[travertine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Travertine \"Travertine\") and marble\nblocks, and nineteen of the originally twenty columns remain but the current\ntile roof was added later. [Palladio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palladio\n\"Palladio\")'s published reconstruction suggested a dome, though this was\napparently erroneous. The temple's original dedication is dated back to circa\n143-132 BC, a time when intense construction was taking place in Portus\nTiberinus.[6]\n\n## Identification\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Temple_of_Hercules_Victor&action=edit&section=2\n\"Edit section: Identification\")]\n\n[![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7b/Et%C3%A0_imperiale%2C_chiusino_a_forma_di_mascherone_di_divinit%C3%A0_fluviale%2C_detta_bocca_della_verit%C3%A0%2C_collocata_qui_nel_1632.jpg/250px-\nEt%C3%A0_imperiale%2C_chiusino_a_forma_di_mascherone_di_divinit%C3%A0_fluviale%2C_detta_bocca_della_verit%C3%A0%2C_collocata_qui_nel_1632.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Et\u00e0_imperiale,_chiusino_a_forma_di_mascherone_di_divinit\u00e0_fluviale,_detta_bocca_della_verit\u00e0,_collocata_qui_nel_1632.jpg)The\n[Mouth of Truth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bocca_della_Verit\u00e0 \"Bocca della\nVerit\u00e0\"), believed to have originated in the temple and been moved later to\nthe nearby Basilica of [Santa Maria in\nCosmedin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Maria_in_Cosmedin \"Santa Maria\nin Cosmedin\")\n\nIts major literary sources are two almost identical passages, one Department of Image Collections,[11] National Gallery of\nArt Library.\n\n## Post-Classical history\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Temple_of_Hercules_Victor&action=edit&section=3\n\"Edit section: Post-Classical history\")]\n\n[![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/92/Temple_Hercule_Olivarius_-\n_plan.svg/250px-Temple_Hercule_Olivarius_-\n_plan.svg.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Temple_Hercule_Olivarius_-\n_plan.svg)Map of the temple\n\nIn the 1st century AD, the temple was hit with some sort of disaster as 10\ncolumns were replaced with Luna marble, which is similar to the original but\nnot an exact replica.[12] By 1132, the temple had been [converted to a\nchurch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianised_sites \"Christianised\nsites\"), known as Santo Stefano alle Carozze (St. Stephen 'of the carriages').\nIn 1140, Innocent II converted the temple into a Christian church dedicating\nit to Santo Stefano.[4]\n\nAdditional restorations (and a [fresco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fresco\n\"Fresco\") over the altar) were made in 1475. A plaque in the floor was\ndedicated by [Sixtus IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sixtus_IV \"Sixtus IV\").\nIn the 12th century, the cella wall was replaced with brick faced concrete and\nwindows were added as well.[12]\n\nIn the 17th century, the church was rededicated to _Santa Maria del Sole_\n(\"St. Mary of the Sun\"). The temple and the Temple of Vesta in\n[Tivoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tivoli,_Italy \"Tivoli, Italy\") were an\ninspiration for Bramante's\n[Tempietto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tempietto_del_Bramante \"Tempietto\ndel Bramante\") and other High Renaissance churches of centralized\nplan.[_[citation\nneeded]("}
