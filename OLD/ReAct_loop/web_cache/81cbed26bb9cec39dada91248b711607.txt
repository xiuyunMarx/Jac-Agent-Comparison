It\u2019s where creators share new\nvisions for creative work with the communities that will come together to fund\nthem.\n\nSome of these creators, like [Critical Role](/projects/criticalrole/critical-\nrole-the-legend-of-vox-machina-animated-s), [TLC](/projects/1507621537/tlc-is-\nback-to-make-our-final-album-with-you), and [The Smithsonian\nInstitution](/projects/smithsonian/conserve-dorothys-ruby-slippers) already\nhad huge fanbases. But many projects have been as small-scale as a [limited\nrun of silent meditation vinyls](/projects/silentmeditation/silent-meditation-\non-vinyl) or as up-and-coming as early versions of [Issa Rae's\nInsecure](/projects/1996857943/the-misadventures-of-awkward-black-girl) and\n[Phoebe Waller-Bridge\u2019s Fleabag](/projects/drywrite/fleabag).\n\nNo matter what, creators always control how the work comes together\u2014no\n100-page grant applications, no donors demanding you modify your message, no\nlast-minute edits from investors. When backers chip in funding and help spread\nthe word, they too become part of these independent works.\n\n\u201cKickstarter is one of those platforms that gives you space to work with\npeople who know you, love you, and support you.\u201d\n\n-- De La Soul\n\nOur community.\n\nTapping into our community starts with activating your own. Most successful\nprojects build a snowball effect, winning over friends and early supporters\nwho then share the idea with their networks, and signal their support to the\nwider Kickstarter community. The snowball can get pretty big. Over 26 million\npeople, from every continent on earth, have helped fund Kickstarter projects.\n\nOur mission.\n\nOur mission is to help bring creative projects to life. We believe that art\nand creative expression are essential to a healthy and vibrant society, and\nthe space to create requires protection.\n\nWe don\u2019t want art world elites and entertainment executives to define our\nculture; we want creative people\u2014even those who\u2019ve never made anything\nbefore\u2014to take the wheel. We help creators connect directly with their\ncommunities, putting power where it belongs.\n\nWe are so committed to our mission, we wrote it into our business model. In\n2015 we became a Public Benefit Corporation\u2014a for-profit company that\nprioritizes positive outcomes for society as much as our shareholders. We\nupdated our corporate charter to lay out specific goals and commitments to put\nour values into our operations, promote arts and culture, fight inequality,\nand help creative projects happen.\n\n[**See our charter** ](https://www.kickstarter.com/charter?ref=hello)\n\nOur team.\n\nWe're an independent company of passionate people working together. We spend\nour time designing and building Kickstarter, forging community around creative\nprojects, and supporting the creative ecosystem around us. We\u2019re developers,\ndesigners, support specialists, writers, musicians, painters, poets, gamers,\nrobot-builders\u2014you name it. Over the years, our team has backed more than\n50,000 projects (and launched plenty of our own).\n\n[**Join us** ](https://www.kickstarter.com/jobs?ref=hello)\n\nOur history.\n\nKickstarter launched on April 28, 2009. A lot has happened since.\n\nWe became a [Public Benefit Corporation](/blog/kickstarter-is-now-a-benefit-\ncorporation) to commit to our principles as much as our profits. We launched\n[The Creative Independent](/blog/introducing-the-creative-independent) to\nshare resources and advice for all types of makers. We invited the local\ncommunity into our Brooklyn headquarters with the [Creators-in-Residence\nprogram](https://www.kickstarter.com/creators-in-residence).\n\nProjects won [Grammys](https://mashable.com/2015/02/09/grammys-kickstarter-\nalbums-music/) and [Oscars](/blog/kickstarter-at-the-oscars). [London\u2019s V&A;\nMuseum](/blog/kickstarter-comes-to-londons-vanda-museum) curated a show about\nKickstarter design projects. Hank Willis Thomas and For Freedoms\u2019 [political\nbillboard project across all 50 states](/blog/for-freedoms-and-kickstarter-\nlaunch-a-campaign-for-the-largest-c) became the largest creative collaboration\nin U.S. history. We went [Climate Neutral](/blog/kickstarter-\njoins-50-companies-going-climate-neutral)\u2014we\u2019re doing everything we can to\nbuild a long, healthy future for the creative culture we helped cultivate.\nThis is just the beginning.\n\nOur story,  \nas told by founder Perry Chen\n\n\u201cI was living in New Orleans in late 2001 and I wanted to bring a pair of DJs\ndown to play a show during the 2002 Jazz Fest. I found a great venue and\nreached out to their management, but in the end the show never happened--it\nwas just too much money...\u201d\n\nThe fact that the potential audience had no say in this decision stuck\nuncomfortably in my brain. I thought: \u201cWhat if people could go to a site and\npledge to buy tickets for a show? And if enough money was pledged they would\nbe charged and the show would happen. If not, it wouldn't.\u201d\n\nI loved the idea, but I was focused on making music, not starting an internet\ncompany. Yet slowly over the next few years I started to work on the idea more\nand more. In the spring of 2005 I moved back home to NYC, knowing it would be\nmuch more possible there.\n\nOnce back in New York, I started to try and tackle the next steps: Who could\nbuild the website? How much it would cost? Where could I get money? I talked\nto a bunch of folks and I learned a ton. I planned and planned.\n\nIn the fall of 2005, I met Yancey Strickler, and we became fast friends.\nYancey soon joined me in brainstorming. We bought a whiteboard. We had big\ndreams. I convinced some friends to give us a little bit of money. At some\npoint I made\n[this](https://s3.amazonaws.com/ksr/assets/000/002/455/4366d20f4880caaa63fdc01b4ec25765_large.jpg)\nrough design of the site. Clearly we needed more help.\n\nAbout a year later, I was introduced to Charles Adler, through an old friend.\nThe day after we were introduced, Charles came over to my apartment and he and\nI started working together almost every day. After months and months of\ncollaboration, we ended up with wireframes and specifications for the site.\n\nBut none of us could code. We had a few false starts hiring people to build\nthe site. There were months where not much happened. Charles moved to San\nFrancisco and took some part-time freelance work. Yancey was still at his day\njob. We had this money from our friends and not much was happening. It was\nemotionally draining.\n\nIn the summer of 2008 things finally started to move again. I was introduced\nto Andy Baio, who, though he was living in Portland, started to help us out.\nSoon after, Charles and Andy found a few developers -- including Lance Ivy all\nthe way in Walla Walla, Washington. We were a scattered team that lived\nthrough Skype and email (Charles had moved again, this time to Chicago), but\nwe were finally building -- even as the economy started to collapse.\n\nFinally, on April 28, 2009, we launched Kickstarter to the public. We told as\nmany friends as possible, and Andy announced it on his awesome blog Waxy.org.\nProjects trickled in. Yancey jumped into gear to handle all the new emails\nfrom people actually using (or wanting to use) Kickstarter. It was amazing!\nYou cannot imagine how excited we all were.\n\nThere are so many projects that defined the early days. Designing Obama, Robin\nWrites a Book, and Mysterious Letters were all landmarks. Filmmakers took\ntheir natural-born hustle and wrapped it around our template. People stepped\nup to support projects over and over again. It was thrilling. And then one day\nwe even had an office. In January 2010, nine months after we launched, we\nmoved into a tenement building in the Lower East Side of Manhattan along with\nCassie Marketos and Fred Benenson, our two new teammates.\n\nBut before all that, three weeks after Kickstarter launched, a young singer-\nsongwriter from Athens, GA, launched a project to fund her album, _[Allison\nWeiss was Right All Along](/projects/allisonweiss/help-make-the-new-allison-\nweiss-ep?ref=press_founder)_. Allison was using Kickstarter in the exact way\nwe had always dreamed. Her album was funded in one day. She did a Skype chat\nwith the backer that put her over her goal and posted it for all to see.
