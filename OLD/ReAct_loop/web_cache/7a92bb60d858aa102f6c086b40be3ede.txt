 \n  \n**Han Kang** ([Korean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_language \"Korean\nlanguage\"): \ud55c\uac15; born 27 November 1970[1]) is a South Korean writer. From 2007\nto 2018, she taught creative writing at the [Seoul Institute of the\nArts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul_Institute_of_the_Arts \"Seoul\nInstitute of the Arts\").[2] Han rose to international prominence for her novel\n_[The Vegetarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Vegetarian \"The\nVegetarian\")_ , which became the first [Korean\nlanguage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_language \"Korean language\")\nnovel to win the [International Booker\nPrize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Booker_Prize \"International\nBooker Prize\") for fiction in 2016. Han is the first Asian woman and Korean to\nbe a recipient of the [Nobel Prize in\nLiterature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Literature \"Nobel\nPrize in Literature\"), receiving the award in 2024 in recognition of her\n\"intense poetic prose that confronts historical traumas and exposes the\nfragility of human life\".[3]\n\n## Early life and education\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Han_Kang&action=edit&section=1 \"Edit section: Early\nlife and education\")]\n\nHan Kang was born on 27 November 1970 in\n[Gwangju](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwangju \"Gwangju\"), South Korea.[4]\nAccording to her father, she is named for the [Han\nRiver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_River_\\(Korea\\) \"Han River\n\\(Korea\\)\") ([Korean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_language \"Korean\nlanguage\"): \ud55c\uac15;\n[RR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revised_Romanization_of_Korean \"Revised\nRomanization of Korean\"): _Hangang_).[5] Her family is noted for its literary\nbackground. Her father is novelist [Han Seung-\nwon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Seung-won \"Han Seung-won\"). Her older\nbrother, Han Dong-rim, is also a novelist, while her younger brother, Han\nKang-in, is a novelist and cartoonist.[4]\n\nAt the age of nine, Han moved to [Suyu-ri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suyu-\ndong \"Suyu-dong\") in [Seoul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul \"Seoul\"),\nwhen her father quit his teaching job to become a full-time writer, four\nmonths before the [Gwangju\nUprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwangju_Uprising \"Gwangju Uprising\"),\na pro-democracy movement that ended in the military's massacre of students and\ncivilians. She first learned about the massacre when she was 12, after\ndiscovering at home a secretly circulated memorial album of photographs taken\nby a German journalist, [J\u00fcrgen\nHinzpeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J\u00fcrgen_Hinzpeter \"J\u00fcrgen\nHinzpeter\").[6] This discovery deeply influenced her view on humanity and her\nliterary works.[1][7]\n\nHan's father struggled to make ends meet with his writing career, which\nnegatively impacted his family.
