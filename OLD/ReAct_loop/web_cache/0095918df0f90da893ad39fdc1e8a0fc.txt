the Northwest wing of the Reading Hospital\n\n**Reading Hospital** is a 697-bed [4][non-\nprofit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-profit_hospital \"Non-profit\nhospital\") [teaching hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teaching_hospital\n\"Teaching hospital\") located in [West\nReading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Reading,_Pennsylvania \"West\nReading, Pennsylvania\"),\n[Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania \"Pennsylvania\"). The\nhospital was established in 1867 and is the anchor institution of [Tower\nHealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tower_Health \"Tower Health\").\n\nReading Hospital is a [level I trauma\ncenter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Level_I_trauma_center \"Level I trauma\ncenter\")[5] and certified stroke center.[6] With more than 133,500 annual\nvisits, the [emergency\ndepartment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergency_department \"Emergency\ndepartment\") is the single busiest site in Pennsylvania and the 11th busiest\nnationwide.[7] The hospital operates several [residency training\nprograms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Residency_\\(medicine\\) \"Residency\n\\(medicine\\)\") for newly graduated physicians such as internal medicine,\ninternal medicine osteopathic, transitional year, family medicine, OB/GYN,\nemergency medicine, anesthesiology, general surgery, neurology, podiatric\nsurgery & psychiatry. One year pharmacy residency is also offered. The\nresidency programs are accredited by the [Accreditation Council for Graduate\nMedical\nEducation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accreditation_Council_for_Graduate_Medical_Education\n\"Accreditation Council for Graduate Medical Education\"). The [internal\nmedicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_medicine \"Internal medicine\")\nresidency is also accredited by the [American Osteopathic\nAssociation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Osteopathic_Association\n\"American Osteopathic Association\").\n\n## History\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Reading_Hospital&action=edit&section=1 \"Edit\nsection: History\")]\n\nIn November 1867, physicians with the Reading Medical Association and 16 local\nbusiness leaders developed plans for the area's first hospital. The Reading\nDispensary opened in downtown Reading on Jan. 27, 1868, and moved to northwest\nReading in 1886 under its new name, The Reading Hospital. Public demand for\nhospital care led to constant expansion, resulting in the 1926 relocation to\nthe present 36-acre (15-hectare) site in West Reading.\n\nIn 2017, Reading hospital introduced Tower Health. This included a purchase of\nBrandywine Hospital in Coatesville; Chestnut Hill Hospital, in Philadelphia;\nJennersville Hospital in West Grove; Phoenixville Hospital in Phoenixville;\nand Pottstown Hospital in Pottstown; St. Christopher's Hospital for Children\nin Philadelphia; and 20 urgent care locations across the service area.[8] In\n2019, a 16-bed pediatric emergency department was added.[9]\n\nIn 2017, Reading Hospital treated more than 133,000 people in its emergency\nroom, delivered 3,500 babies, provided more than 750,000 outpatient services\nand 33,000 inpatient admissions. Reading Hospital operates a level I trauma\ncenter.[5] The hospital is certified as an advanced comprehensive stroke\ncenter.[6]\n\nThe campus is a principal teaching site for Drexel University College of\nMedicine. In 2021, Drexel opened a new 4-year medical campus in West Reading.\nStudents at the Drexel College of Medicine at Tower Health have the\nopportunity to complete all 4 years of the MD program at Reading Hospital.[10]\n\n## Awards and recognition\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Reading_Hospital&action=edit&section=2 \"Edit\nsection: Awards and recognition\")]\n\n  * From 2022 to 2025, Healthgrades designated Reading Hospital as one of America's 50 Best Hospitals. Reading was also named one of America's 100 best for stroke care and joint replacement. Individual areas recognized within the top 10% include cardiac surgery, neurosciences, joint replacement, overall pulmonary treatment, and GI medical treatment. It is within the top 5% for treatment of stroke.[11]\n  * U.S. News & World Report recognized Reading Hospital as a 2022-2023 best hospital, and ranked #8 in Pennsylvania. Reading was high performing in 5 specialties including gastroenterology, geriatrics, orthopedics, pulmonary and lung surgery, and urology. The hospital also received high performing marks in COPD, colon cancer surgery, diabetes, heart attack, heart failure, hip replacement, kidney failure, knee replacement, pneumonia, and stroke.[12]\n  * Since 2016, Reading Hospital has received the prestigious MAGNET designation for nursing excellence by the American Nurses Credentialing Center (ANCC).[13]\n\n## Graduate medical education\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Reading_Hospital&action=edit&section=3 \"Edit\nsection: Graduate medical education\")]\n\nReading Hospital operates a number of\n[residency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Residency_\\(medicine\\) \"Residency\n\\(medicine\\)\") training programs for newly graduated\n[physicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicians \"Physicians\"). Programs\ninclude Emergency Medicine, [Family\nMedicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_Medicine \"Family Medicine\"),\n[Internal Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_Medicine \"Internal\nMedicine\"), [OB/GYN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OB/GYN \"OB/GYN\"), Podiatric\nMedicine and Surgery, [Psychiatry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychiatry\n\"Psychiatry\"), and a Transitional Year.[14] All programs are accredited by the\n[Accreditation Council for Graduate Medical\nEducation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accreditation_Council_for_Graduate_Medical_Education\n\"Accreditation Council for Graduate Medical Education\") (ACGME). The internal\nmedicine program is also dually accredited by the [American Osteopathic\nAssociation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Osteopathic_Association\n\"American Osteopathic Association\").[15] There is also a residency programs\nfor pharmacists.[14] In January 2021, Tower Health announced additional\nresidency programs in [neurology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neurology\n\"Neurology\") and [physical medicine and\nrehabilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_medicine_and_rehabilitation\n\"Physical medicine and rehabilitation\"), as well as fellowship programs in\nnephrology, plastic surgery, sleep medicine, and pulmonary disease and\ncritical care medicine.[16]\n\n##
