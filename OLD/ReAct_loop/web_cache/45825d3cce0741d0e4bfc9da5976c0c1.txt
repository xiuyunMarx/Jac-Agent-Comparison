Fritz added: \"Penske Media is the first major U.S. news company to\nchallenge Google and its parent Alphabet in court over its growing use of\nartificial intelligence, which many publishers have said is damaging their\nbusinesses. Penske filed the antitrust suit late Friday in federal district\ncourt in the District of Columbia, where a judge last year found Google has an\nillegal monopoly in internet search. That judge earlier this month imposed\nsofter penalties than the government had been seeking, in part because of the\ngrowing competition Google faces from other AI companies. Online education\ncompany Chegg has also sued Google over its AI Overviews in D.C. district\ncourt, as has a small Arkansas newspaper, the Helena World Chronicle, in a\nproposed class-action case.\" Alongside its flagship Gemini chatbot, Google\n\"has been integrating AI more prominently into its search results\" for the\npast sixteen months; the vast majority of queries \"now produce an 'AI\nOverview' with information related to a search or question above links to\nother sites,\" while users may elect to use an AI Mode in lieu of traditional\nsearch per Fritz. He continued: \"AI summaries and AI Mode include links to\nwebsites where the information was found. But publishers including Penske have\nsaid consumers often have little need to follow the links because the\ninformation they need is in the AI-generated answer. Penske said in its\ncomplaint that about 20% of Google search results with a link to one of its\nsites include AI Overviews and that percentage has been rising. It also said\nrevenue on its sites from affiliate links for online shopping have dropped by\nmore than a third since the end of 2024, which it attributed to decreased\ntraffic from Google.\" In its complaint, Penske has further alleged that\n\"siphoning and discouraging user traffic to [...] websites in this manner will\nhave profoundly harmful effects on the overall quality and quantity of the\ninformation accessible on the internet.\" Jos\u00e9 Casta\u00f1eda, a spokesperson for\nthe high-technology behemoth, has maintained that \"AI Overviews send traffic\nto a greater diversity of sites [...] We will defend against these meritless\nclaims,\" also asserting to Fritz that \"clicks on links that accompany AI\nOverviews are higher quality for publishers because [...] those users spend\nmore time on the site.\" However, the complaint alleged that \"with every\narticle it publishes on its websites, PMC is forced to provide Google with\nmore training and grounding material for its [AI] systems to generate AI\nOverviews or refine its models, adding fuel to a fire that threatens PMC's\nentire publishing business.\" The lawsuit, which seeks a permanent injunction\nagainst Google for its actions alongside \"unspecified monetary damages,\"\nincludes thirteen of the Penske publications as plaintiffs, although the\npredominantly Penske-owned Vox Media is not a party to the lawsuit.\n\nSeptember 15, 2025,\n\n[The Wall Street Journal](https://www.wsj.com/tech/ai/rolling-stone-publisher-\nsues-google-over-ai-summaries-3afde408)\n\n__\n\n##  [Israel strike kills Al Jazeera Al Jazeera, which\nis funded by the Qatari government, rejected the claim and, before his death,\nAl Sharif also had denied Israeli charges against him. Al Sharif, 28, was\namong four Al Jazeera journalists and an assistant who died in an airstrike on\na tent near Al Shifa Hospital in eastern Gaza City, Gaza officials and Al\nJazeera said. A hospital official said two other people died. A sixth\njournalist, local freelance reporter Mohammad Al-Khaldi, was also killed in\nthe strike, medics at Al Shifa Hospital said on Monday. In a statement, Al\nJazeera lauded Al Sharif as \"one of Gaza's bravest journalists,\" asserting\nthat the attack was a \"desperate attempt to silence voices in anticipation of\nthe occupation of Gaza.\" Other journalists killed in the attack include\nMohammed Qreiqeh, Ibrahim Zaher and Mohammed Noufal, according to Al Jazeera.\n\"The deliberate targeting of journalists by Israel in the Gaza Strip reveals\nhow these crimes are beyond imagination,\" Qatari Prime Minister Sheikh\nMohammed bin Abdulrahman Al-Thani said in a post on X, while British Prime\nMinister Keir Starmer said he is \"'gravely concerned' about the repeated\ntargeting of journalists in Gaza,\u201d according to a Reuters report. Israeli military spokesperson Avichay Adraee posted photos on X\nthat appeared to show Al Sharif with Yahya Sinwar, the mastermind of the Hamas\nOctober 2023 attack on Israel. Moore](https://www.pulitzer.org/board/2013) was inducted into the\nNational Association of Black Journalists' (NABJ) Hall of Fame at a luncheon\ntoday under the aegis of the #NABJ25 Convention & Career Fair in Cleveland,\naccording to the educational and professional organization. In his\nprofessional career, Moore co-founded and has served as chief executive of\nKlowtify, a media services firm. He previously served as a top executive at\nThe Expert Press, a Canadian-based content agency that crafts expert\ncommentaries for the American press. His journalism career culminated in a\n14-year stint as executive editor of The Denver Post, where he oversaw a range\nof Pulitzer-winning journalism, perhaps most notably the news organization's\n[2013 Breaking News Reporting Prize-winning coverage of the 2012 Aurora, Colo.\ncinema mass shooting that killed 12 and injured 58](https://Aurora, Colo.,\nthat killed 12 and injured 58). Before joining The Post, he spent 16 years at\nThe Boston Globe, leaving as managing editor. A native of Cleveland and the\neldest of five siblings, Moore spent his early career in his home state after\nreceiving his undergraduate degree in journalism and political science from\nOhio Wesleyan University. He has been a recipient of NABJ\u2019s Lifetime\nAchievement Award and the Benjamin C. Bradlee Editor of the Year Award from\nthe National Press Foundation in addition to serving as a board member and\ntrustee of myriad organizations, including NABJ, the now-defunct American\nSociety of News Editors, the University of Denver, the Boettcher Foundation\nand Polaris, a Washington, D.C.-based anti-human trafficking organization.\nSince leaving The Post, he has remained in the Denver metropolitan area with\nhis family; in 2023, he was the co-chair of incumbent Denver Mayor Mike\nJohnston's transition team.
