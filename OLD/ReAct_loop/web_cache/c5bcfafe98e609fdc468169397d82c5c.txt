Circus\nMaximus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regio_XI_Circus_Maximus \"Regio XI\nCircus Maximus\"), [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome \"Rome\"), Italy  \nHistory  \nBuilt| c. 120\u201380 BCE  \nSite notes  \nArea| [Forum Boarium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forum_Boarium \"Forum\nBoarium\")  \n  \nThe **Temple of Portunus**\n([Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_language \"Italian language\"):\n_Tempio di Portuno_) is a small ancient [Roman\ntemple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_temple \"Roman temple\") in\n[Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome \"Rome\"), Italy, on the modern\n[Piazza della Bocca della\nVerit\u00e0](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piazza_della_Bocca_della_Verit\u00e0 \"Piazza\ndella Bocca della Verit\u00e0\") ([Rione XII,\nRipa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ripa_\\(rione_of_Rome\\) \"Ripa \\(rione of\nRome\\)\")). It was built beside the ancient [Forum\nBoarium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forum_Boarium \"Forum Boarium\"), the\nRoman cattle market associated with\n[Hercules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hercules \"Hercules\"), which was\nadjacent to Rome's oldest river port (_Portus Tiberinus_) and the oldest stone\n[bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_bridge \"Roman bridge\") across the\n[Tiber River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiber_River \"Tiber River\"), the\n[Pons Aemilius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pons_Aemilius \"Pons Aemilius\").\nIt is theorized that it was dedicated to the Roman god\n[Portunus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portunus_\\(mythology\\) \"Portunus\n\\(mythology\\)\") although the precise dedication remains unclear as there were\nseveral temples nearby also with ambiguous dedications. It was misidentified\nas the **Temple of Fortuna Virilis**\n([Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_language \"Latin language\") for\n\"Manly Luck\") from the\n[Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Renaissance \"Italian\nRenaissance\") and remains better known by this name. The temple is one of the\nbest preserved of all Roman temples.\n\n[![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c7/Rome.Temple_of_Portunus.backside.JPG/250px-\nRome.Temple_of_Portunus.backside.JPG?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Rome.Temple_of_Portunus.backside.JPG)Rear\nview\n\nIt is the most prominent temple in Rome dedicated to Portunus, the god of\nkeys, doors and livestock, and granaries.[1] During the [Middle\nAges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages \"Middle Ages\"), the temple was\nconverted to a Christian\n[church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_\\(building\\) \"Church\n\\(building\\)\") dedicated to _Santa Maria Egyziaca_ or Saint [Mary of\nEgypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_of_Egypt \"Mary of Egypt\"), which was\nthe national church for the [Armenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenia\n\"Armenia\") community in Rome. The legend on [Giovanni Battista\nPiranesi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Battista_Piranesi \"Giovanni\nBattista Piranesi\")'s _[veduta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veduta\n\"Veduta\")_ of about 1770 says it had the popular name of the \"House of\nPilate\".[2] It remained an active church until the early 20th century, when it\nwas [deconsecrated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deconsecration\n\"Deconsecration\"), stripped of all surrounding medieval and Renaissance era\nbuildings, and returned to its classical appearance. It is not ordinarily open\nto the public, though the exterior can be seen from fairly close up.\n\n## Architecture\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Temple_of_Portunus&action=edit&section=1 \"Edit\nsection: Architecture\")]\n\n[![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2b/Details_van_de_Tempel_van_Portunus_te_Rome_Dimostrazione_in_grande_delle_parti_del_tempio_della_Fortuna_Virile_%28titel_op_object%29_Le_Antichit%C3%A0_Romane_%28serietitel%29%2C_RP-\nP-OB-36.889_%28cropped%29.jpg/250px-\nthumbnail.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Details_van_de_Tempel_van_Portunus_te_Rome_Dimostrazione_in_grande_delle_parti_del_tempio_della_Fortuna_Virile_\\(titel_op_object\\)_Le_Antichit\u00e0_Romane_\\(serietitel\\),_RP-\nP-OB-36.889_\\(cropped\\).jpg)Architectural details by [Giovanni Battista\nPiranesi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Battista_Piranesi \"Giovanni\nBattista Piranesi\")\n\nIt is in the [Ionic order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ionic_order \"Ionic\norder\") and is by the ancient Forum Boarium by the\n[Tiber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiber \"Tiber\"). During\n[Antiquity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_antiquity \"Classical\nantiquity\") the site overlooked the Tiberine port at a sharp bend in the\nriver; from here, Portunus watched over cattle barges as they entered the city\nfrom\n[Ostia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostia_Antica_\\(archaeological_site\\)\n\"Ostia Antica \\(archaeological site\\)\").[3]\n\nThe temple was originally built in the 3rd or 4th century BC and was rebuilt\nbetween 120 and 80 BC.[4] The rectangular building consists of a\n[tetrastyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetrastyle \"Tetrastyle\")\n[portico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portico \"Portico\") and\n[cella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cella \"Cella\"), raised on a high\n[podium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Podium \"Podium\") reached by a flight of\nsteps, which it retains.[5] Like the [Maison\nCarr\u00e9e](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maison_Carr\u00e9e \"Maison Carr\u00e9e\") in\n[N\u00eemes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N\u00eemes \"N\u00eemes\"), it has a\n_[pronaos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pronaos \"Pronaos\")_\n[portico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portico \"Portico\") of four [Ionic\ncolumns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ionic_column \"Ionic column\") across and\ntwo columns deep. The columns of the portico are free-standing, while the\nremaining five columns on the long sides and the four columns at the rear are\nhalf-columns [engaged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engaged_column \"Engaged\ncolumn\") along the walls of the cella. This form is sometimes called\n_[pseudoperipteral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudoperipteral\n\"Pseudoperipteral\")_ , as distinct from a true\n[peripteral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peripteral \"Peripteral\") temple\nlike the [Parthenon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parthenon \"Parthenon\")\nentirely surrounded by free-standing columns. The Ionic capitals are of the\noriginal form, different in the frontal and side views, except in the volutes\nat the corners, which project at 45\u00b0, a common Roman detail. It is built of\n[tuff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuff \"Tuff\") and\n[travertine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Travertine \"Travertine\") with a\n[stucco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stucco \"Stucco\") surface.\n\nIf still in use by the 4th-century, the temple would have been closed during\nthe [persecution of pagans in the late Roman\nEmpire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persecution_of_pagans_in_the_late_Roman_Empire\n\"Persecution of pagans in the late Roman Empire\").
