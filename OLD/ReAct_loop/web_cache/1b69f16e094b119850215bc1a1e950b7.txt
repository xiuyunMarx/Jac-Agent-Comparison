-59.417\nArea\n242\nsq\nmi (630\nkm\n2\n)\nEstablished\n1929\nGoverning\nbody\nProtected Areas Trust\nGolden rocket frog\n,  Kaieteur National Park.\nKaieteur National Park\nis a\nnational park\nlocated in the\nPotaro-Siparuni\nRegion of\nGuyana\n, roughly 633 km (393.33 miles) south of\nGeorgetown\n, the nation's capital, closer to the border with\nBrazil\nthan to the\nCaribbean\ncoast. It is widely considered the country's only national park,\n[\n1\n]\n[\n2\n]\nas the capital's\nNational Park\nis a not a true\nwilderness reserve\n. Kaieteur is part of the\nGuianan moist forests\necoregion.\n[\n3\n]\nThe main tourist attraction in the park is\nKaieteur Falls\n, considered the largest single-drop\nwaterfall\nanywhere on earth, by\nvolume\nof water.\n[\n4\n]\nThe Kaieteur National Park Act was enacted by the government to protect the region's wealth of natural resources, its unique natural features (such as the aforementioned Kaieteur Falls), its\nbiodiversity\nof Amazonian flora and fauna, and for the several\nAmerindian\ntribes whom have lived and thrived in this forest for millennia.\n[\n4\n]\nThe Act is administered by the Kaieteur National Park Commission. There are organisms unique to this region that cannot be found anywhere else on earth, such as the golden rocket frog (\nAnomaloglossus beebei\n), a diminutive\nfrog\nthat only breeds within the leaves of the\nbromeliad\nspecies\nBrocchinia micrantha\n(within the park), and nowhere else in the world.\nThe region is served by\nKaieteur International Airport\n, which is situated at Kaieteur Falls.\nBoundaries\n[\nedit\n]\nIn 1999 the park's area was increased from\n5 square miles (10\nkm\n2\n)\nto\n242 square miles (630\nkm\n2\n)\nby a Presidential Order.\nMining\n[\nedit\n]\nIn 2017, two dozen villagers from the Chenapau community were arrested for illegal\nmining\nwithin the Kaieteur National Park boundaries but were later released without any charges.\n[\n5\n]\nIn November 2023, villagers were again accused of illegal mining within the park boundaries, which led to a protest by the villagers voicing their need to mine as a main source of income. The Chief Warden and the Guyana Geology and Mines Commission (GGMC) ordered the protesters to leave or face consequences. The villagers claimed to be mining in a\nbuffer zone\nthat was marked as land to be used for mining in accordance with the Amerindian Act.\n[\n5\n]\nMinister of Natural Resources Vickram Bharrat claimed that the GGMC will help the villagers find another area to mine that does not include boundaries within the National Park.\n[\n5\n]\nReferences\n[\nedit\n]\n\u2191\nBBC Wildlife Magazine\n.
