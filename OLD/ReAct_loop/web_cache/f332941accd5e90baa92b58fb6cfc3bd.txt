*\n[Italy: Central](https://lionsinthepiazza.com/category/destinations/italy-\ncentral/)\n\n# Temple of Hercules Victor: The Oldest Building in Rome\n\nPosted onMarch 5, 2023August 3, 2026 Updated onAugust 3, 2026 [Ancient Greek &\nRoman](https://lionsinthepiazza.com/category/architecture/greco-roman/),\n[Italy: Central](https://lionsinthepiazza.com/category/destinations/italy-\ncentral/)\n\nThe oldest building in Rome doesn\u2019t call much attention to itself. For a\nculture which showed the world how to build big, it stands at a mere ten and a\nhalf meters. Instead of occupying a lofty hilltop or an illustrious avenue, it\nsits in the middle of an old cattle market. It doesn\u2019t have any inscriptions\nproclaiming the glory of its patron. For centuries, it was confused with a\ndifferent building entirely \u2013 and even today, its identification remains more\ntheory than fact.\n\nYet the round structure in [the Forum\nBoarium](https://lionsinthepiazza.com/forum-boarium/) \u2013 now known as the\nTemple of Hercules Victor \u2013 has survived for over two thousand years. It\u2019s a\nmonument to Rome\u2019s Republican years \u2013 before the megalomaniac emperors \u2013 and a\ntribute to the refinement of Greek culture. In a city where pomp and grandeur\nreigned supreme, this little building holds its own by virtue of its elegant\nlines. It\u2019s also remained remarkably intact, even compared to Roman structures\nbuilt hundreds of years later.\n\n![Rome Temple Of Hercules Victor Side View Forum Boarium Greek Columns Round\nClassical Architecture](https://lionsinthepiazza.com/wp-\ncontent/uploads/2023/03/rome-hercules-1024x874.jpg)\n\nThe temple hides in plain sight, by the river\u2019s bank between the Circus\nMaximus and Tiber Island. We stumbled across it more than once without knowing\nwhat it was. It sits in the middle of the Forum Boarium, a park-like block at\nthe foot of the Palatine Bridge. Across the street, busloads of tourists line\nup to see the Mouth of Truth, all of them seemingly unaware of one of Rome\u2019s\nmost remarkable structures.\n\n## The Round Temple in the Forum Boarium\n\nRome\u2019s first set of docks, the Portus Tiberius, clustered around a flat area\nwhere the Capitoline, Palatine, and Aventine hills meet. The surrounding site\ngrew into the Forum Boarium, a thriving market area where cattle and other\ngoods entered the city. Trading activities in turn prompted the construction\nof temples where merchants could seek the gods\u2019 blessing.\n\n![Rome Forum Boarium Ancient Temple Hercules Victor Greek Columns Round Tholos\nClassical Architecture Morning Light Trees](https://lionsinthepiazza.com/wp-\ncontent/uploads/2023/03/rome-forum-boarium-hercules-1024x762.jpg)\n\nAfter much debate, most scholars now date the circular temple to the second\ncentury BCE. 19 of the original 20 columns survive, although about half the\ncapitals were probably replaced in the first century. In spite of its\ndiminutive size, the papacy ordered the temple consecrated as a church in\n1140. Originally dedicated to Santo Stefano and later to Santa Maria del Sole,\nthe structure picked up windows and some interior frescoes over the years. No\none seems to know when the roof disappeared or what the original looked like.\n\nVestal Virgins famously worshipped in round temples, and for centuries most\npeople assumed this one had been dedicated to goddess Vesta. By the time of\nNapoleon\u2019s invasion, more excavations and written sources indicated Hercules\ninstead. For more on Hercules in ancient Rome, see our post on the [Forum\nBoarium](https://lionsinthepiazza.com/forum-boarium/).\n\n## A Roman Temple in the Greek Style\n\nOther than its circular form, the temple\u2019s most distinctive aspect is its pure\nGreek style, right down to its walls of solid marble imported from Attica.\nMost structures in Rome adapted Hellenistic architecture rather than copying\nit outright, especially when it came to using stone. [Pragmatic Romans\npreferred to build with lightweight, locally-available\nmaterials](https://lionsinthepiazza.com/ancient-architecture-\nrome/#construction) like volcanic tufa and brick, limiting marble to a thin\nlayer on top. The unusual and extravagant all-marble construction of the\nTemple of Hercules required labor-intensive carving for each and every piece.\n\n![Rome Temple Of Hercules Victor Second Century BCE Greek Marble Corinthian\nColumns And Roof Detail Forum Boarium](https://lionsinthepiazza.com/wp-\ncontent/uploads/2023/03/rome-temple-detail-1024x768.jpg)\n\nAlthough scholars continue to debate the temple\u2019s origins, one prominent\ntheory links it with a second-century BCE general who conquered much of\nGreece. Lucius Mummius Achaicus served as both consul and censor \u2013 the most\npowerful positions of the Roman Republic \u2013 during years of intense\nconstruction in and around the Forum Boarium. Sponsoring a costly temple in\nthe area would help him compete with his aristocratic co-censor and rival\nScipio Aemilianus Africanus. Its Greek style would remind Romans of Mummius\u2019\nvictory, right down to the Corinthian capitals evoking the city he sacked.\n\n## Renaissance Legacy\n\n![Rome Tempietto At San Pietro Montorio Renaissance Temple By Bramante Round\nChurch With Columns And Dome](https://lionsinthepiazza.com/wp-\ncontent/uploads/2023/03/rome-tempietto-738x1024.jpg) ![Rome Bramante Tempietto\nRenaissance Church Interior Blue Dome Classical\nArchitecture](https://lionsinthepiazza.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/rome-\ntempietto-dome-756x1024.jpg)\n\nRenaissance architect Bramante\u2019s iconic Tempietto was inspired by the Temple\nof Hercules Victor.\n\nThe round temple inspired many Renaissance architects, although they often\nadded domes to their interpretations. Presumably they were unaware that the\ntemple pre-dates Roman domes. Palladio\u2019s sketch of the temple includes a dome,\nas does Bramante\u2019s influential Tempietto in Trastevere.\n\n## Further Reading\n\nMary Beard\u2019s [SPQR: A History of
