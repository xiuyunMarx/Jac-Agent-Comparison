**\u00b7**\n[2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Nobel_Prize_in_Literature \"2025\nNobel Prize in Literature\") \u2192  \n  \nIn 2024, the [Nobel Prize in\nLiterature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Literature \"Nobel\nPrize in Literature\") was awarded to the South Korean author [Han\nKang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Kang \"Han Kang\") (born 1970) \"for her\nintense poetic prose that confronts historical traumas and exposes the\nfragility of human life\". It was announced by the [Swedish\nAcademy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Academy \"Swedish Academy\") in\n[Stockholm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm \"Stockholm\"),\n[Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden \"Sweden\"), on 10 October 2024\nand was awarded on 10 December 2024.[1]\n\nShe is the [first South\nKorean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Korean_Nobel_laureates_and_nominees\n\"List of Korean Nobel laureates and nominees\") and [first Asian\nwoman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Asian_Nobel_laureates \"List of\nAsian Nobel laureates\") to win the Nobel Prize in Literature, making her the\n18th [woman to win the Nobel Prize in\nLiterature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_female_Nobel_laureates \"List\nof female Nobel laureates\") and the tenth [Asian laureate in that Nobel Prize\ncategory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Asian_Nobel_laureates#Literature\n\"List of Asian Nobel laureates\").[2]\n\n## Laureate\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2024_Nobel_Prize_in_Literature&action=edit&section=1\n\"Edit section: Laureate\")]\n\nMain article: [Han Kang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Kang \"Han Kang\")\n\nHan Kang grew up with a literary background in\n[Seoul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul \"Seoul\"), her father, [Han Seung-\nwon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Seung-won \"Han Seung-won\"), being a\nreputed novelist. Alongside her passion for writing, she spends time exploring\narts and music, which is reflected throughout her literary production. She\nstarted her career in 1993 with the publication of her poems in the literary\nmagazine _Literature and Society_ , and had her debut prose publication in\n1995 with the short story collection _Love of Yeosu_ (\uc5ec\uc218\uc758 \uc0ac\ub791). Her major\nbreakthrough came with the 2007 novel _[The\nVegetarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Vegetarian \"The Vegetarian\")_ ,\na book that portrays the violent consequences that ensue when its protagonist\nsuddenly begins to refuse to eat meat. Her experimental and often disturbing\nstories are characterized by a double exposure of pain and a correspondence\nbetween mental and physical torment, confronting historical traumas and\ninvisible sets of rules and the fragility of human life. Her other well-known\nworks include _[Human Acts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Acts \"Human\nActs\")_ , _[The White Book](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_White_Book \"The\nWhite Book\")_ , _[Greek Lessons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Lessons\n\"Greek Lessons\")_ , and _[We Do Not\nPart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/We_Do_Not_Part \"We Do Not Part\")_.[3][4]\n\n## Candidates\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2024_Nobel_Prize_in_Literature&action=edit&section=2\n\"Edit section: Candidates\")]\n\nOn [Ladbrokes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ladbrokes \"Ladbrokes\"), the top\nfavourites to be awarded the 2024 Nobel Prize in Literature were Chinese\nauthor [Can Xue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Can_Xue \"Can Xue\"), Australian\n[Gerald Murnane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerald_Murnane \"Gerald\nMurnane\"), Japanese [Haruki\nMurakami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haruki_Murakami \"Haruki Murakami\"),\nCanadians [Margaret Atwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margaret_Atwood\n\"Margaret Atwood\") and [Anne Carson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne_Carson\n\"Anne Carson\"), American [Thomas\nPynchon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Pynchon \"Thomas Pynchon\") and\nArgentinian [C\u00e9sar Aira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C\u00e9sar_Aira \"C\u00e9sar\nAira\"). Han Kang's odds to win the prize were 33/1, behind perennial\nfavourites such as Kenyan [Ngugi Wa\nThiong\u2019o](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ngugi_Wa_Thiong\u2019o \"Ngugi Wa\nThiong\u2019o\"), Indian-born British-American [Salman\nRushdie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salman_Rushdie \"Salman Rushdie\"),\nHungarians [P\u00e9ter N\u00e1das](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P\u00e9ter_N\u00e1das \"P\u00e9ter\nN\u00e1das\") and [L\u00e1szl\u00f3\nKrasznahorkai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L\u00e1szl\u00f3_Krasznahorkai \"L\u00e1szl\u00f3\nKrasznahorkai\"), Romanian [Mircea\nC\u0103rt\u0103rescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mircea_C\u0103rt\u0103rescu \"Mircea\nC\u0103rt\u0103rescu\"), Russian [Lyudmila\nUlitskaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyudmila_Ulitskaya \"Lyudmila\nUlitskaya\"), American [Don DeLillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_DeLillo\n\"Don DeLillo\") and French [Michel\nHouellebecq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michel_Houellebecq \"Michel\nHouellebecq\").[5]\n\n## Prize announcement\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2024_Nobel_Prize_in_Literature&action=edit&section=3\n\"Edit section: Prize announcement\")]\n\n[![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/76/Han_Kang_Vegetarian_2015_title.jpg/250px-\nHan_Kang_Vegetarian_2015_title.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Han_Kang_Vegetarian_2015_title.jpg)\n\n[![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/65/HanKang.jpg/250px-\nHanKang.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:HanKang.jpg)\n\nIn 2016, Han Kang's novel _[The\nVegetarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Vegetarian \"The Vegetarian\")_\n[\ucc44\uc2dd\uc8fc\uc758\uc790] that was translated into English by [Deborah\nSmith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deborah_Smith_\\(translator\\) \"Deborah\nSmith \\(translator\\)\") won the [International Booker\nPrize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Booker_Prize \"International\nBooker Prize\").\n\nAfter the announcement, [Anders\nOlsson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anders_Olsson_\\(writer\\) \"Anders Olsson\n\\(writer\\)\"), chairman of the Academy's Nobel Committee, said in a statement:\n\n> \"She has a unique awareness of the connections between body and soul, the\n> living and the dead, and in her poetic and experimental style has become an\n> innovator in contemporary prose.\"[6]\n\n[Anna-Karin Palm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna-Karin_Palm \"Anna-Karin\nPalm\") was interviewed by Carin Klaesson, a correspondent of the Nobel Prize\nMuseum, and described Han Kang's writing with the following statement:\n\n> \"I would say this is a very rich and complex oeuvre that spans many genres,\n> and [she] writes really intense lyrical prose that is both tender and brutal\n> and, sometimes, slightly surrealistic as well... There's a continuity as to\n> themes that is quite remarkable but at the same time a huge stylistic\n> variation that makes every book a new aspect or a new expression of these\n> central themes.\"[7]\n\n## Reactions\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2024_Nobel_Prize_in_Literature&action=edit&section=4\n\"Edit section: Reactions\")]\n\n### Personal reactions\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2024_Nobel_Prize_in_Literature&action=edit&section=5\n\"Edit section: Personal reactions\")]\n\nHan Kang had just finished dinner with her son at her home in\n[Seoul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul \"Seoul\") when she received the\ninformation. Both she and her son were so surprised that they did not even\nhave the time to properly discuss it. She stated that she was \"honored\" to\nreceive the prize. Being the first Nobel laureate in literature in South\nKorea, she said:\n\n> \"Yes. I grew up with books, you know, so since when I was a child, I grew up\n> with books in Korean and translated as well. So I can say I grew up with\n> Korean literature, which I feel very close to. So I hope this news is nice\n> for Korean literature readers and my friends, writers... For me, since when\n> I was a child, all writers have been collective. They are searching meanings\n> in life. Sometimes they are lost and sometimes they are determined and all\n> their efforts and all their strengths have been my inspiration. So it's very\n> difficult for me to pick some names of the inspiration. It's very difficult\n> for me.\"[8]\n\nAsked by Nobel correspondent, Jenny Ryd\u00e9n, where someone just discovering her\nwork should start, she suggested:\n\n> \"I think every writer likes his or her most recent book. So my most recent\n> book is _We Do Not Part_ or it is called _I Do Not Bid Farewell_ or\n> _Impossible Goodbyes_. I hope this book could be a start. And _Human Acts_\n> is connected directly with this book _We Do Not Part_. And then _The White\n> Book_ , which is very personal book for me. Because it's quite\n> autobiographical. And there is _The Vegetarian_. But I feel the start could\n> be _We Do Not Part_.[8]\n\nLater on, Han Kang said her celebrations would be low-key: \"After this phone\ncall I'd like to have tea with - I don't drink so - I'm going to have tea with\nmy son and I'll celebrate it quietly tonight.\"[6][8]\n\n### International reactions\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2024_Nobel_Prize_in_Literature&action=edit&section=6\n\"Edit section: International reactions\")]\n\nDiscussing the choice of Han Kang as the Nobel laureate on [Sveriges\nTelevision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sveriges_Television \"Sveriges\nTelevision\") minutes after the announcement, a panel of the Swedish literature\ncritics Ingrid Elam, Jonas Thente and author Lyra Ekstr\u00f6m Lindb\u00e4ck disagreed\non the Swedish Academy's choice. Elam said it was a very good choice, calling\nHan Kang \"a fantastic author\", while the others had not been impressed by her\nwriting. \"Nobody is going to remember her in fifty years' time\", Thente\nsaid.[9]\n\nA negative reaction also appeared in\n_[Expressen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expressen \"Expressen\")_ by\nliterature critic Victor Malm who called it \"a rather weak choice\".[10]\n_[Svenska Dagbladet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Svenska_Dagbladet \"Svenska\nDagbladet\")'_s literature editor Josefin de Gregorio was pleased with the\nchoice, praising Han Kang for her \"dynamic, transgressive writing that\nportrays deep evil and great trauma with lyrical beauty and vulnerability\" and\nthe Academy's choice of a young and popular writer.[11] Irish novelist [Eimear\nMcBride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eimear_McBride \"Eimear McBride\") was\nalso pleased, calling Han Kang \"one of the greatest living writers\"; \u201cShe is a\nvoice for women, for truth and, above all, for the power of what literature\ncan be. This is a very richly deserved win.\u201d[12]\n\nThe choice of Han Kang was well received by several German commentators, who\nfound it a good choice and positive that the Swedish Academy after heavy focus\non European literature in recent years had chosen a writer from another part\nof the world. \"One can call it strange for an award that aims to do\ncomparative justice to all national literatures that so far [so few\nAsian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Asian_Nobel_laureates#Literature\n\"List of Asian Nobel laureates\") or\n[African](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_African_Nobel_laureates \"List\nof African Nobel laureates\") authors have been given consideration\u201d, Andreas\nPlatthaus in the [Frankfurter Allgemeine\nZeitung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurter_Allgemeine_Zeitung\n\"Frankfurter Allgemeine Zeitung\") wrote.[13]\n\n#### South Korean reactions\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2024_Nobel_Prize_in_Literature&action=edit&section=7\n\"Edit section: South Korean reactions\")]\n\nWhile visiting Laos for a meeting of Asian leaders, South Korean president\n[Yoon Suk Yeol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoon_Suk_Yeol \"Yoon Suk Yeol\")\nposted on his [Facebook account](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facebook\n\"Facebook\") his warmest greetings, saying:\n\n> \"It is a monumental achievement in the history of [Korean\n> literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_literature \"Korean\n> literature\") and a national celebration that brings joy to all the people.\n> You have transformed the painful wounds of our modern history into a great\n> literary work. I extend my deepest respect to you for elevating the value of\n> Korean literature.\"[14][15]\n\n[BTS member](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BTS \"BTS\")\n[V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V_\\(singer\\) \"V \\(singer\\)\") shared in the\nnews on [Instagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instagram \"Instagram\"),\nwriting: \"I read your book 'Human Act: A Novel' in the army. Congratulations\"\nalongside a bowing [emoji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emoji \"Emoji\").[15]\nThe literary critic Kim Seong-shin expressed that the Academy's choice of Han\nwas beyond reproach, affirming: \"I'd say she is the most deserving Korean\nnovelist to be awarded the Nobel Prize.\"[15]\n\nA number of South Korean writers celebrated the news, among them [Min Jin\nLee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Min_Jin_Lee \"Min Jin Lee\"), author of\n_[Pachinko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pachinko_\\(novel\\) \"Pachinko\n\\(novel\\)\")_ , who commented: \"Han Kang is a brilliant novelist who reflects\nour modern condition with courage, imagination, and intelligence. She is most\nworthy of this global recognition.\"[1"}
