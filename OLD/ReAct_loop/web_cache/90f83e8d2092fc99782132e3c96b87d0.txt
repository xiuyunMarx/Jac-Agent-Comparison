in\n[Fiction](/prize-winners-by-category/219)\n\nFor distinguished fiction published during the year by an American author,\npreferably dealing with American life, Fifteen thousand dollars ($15,000).\n\n#  _Night Watch_ , by Jayne Anne Phillips (Knopf)\n\nA beautifully rendered novel set in West Virginia\u2019s Trans-Allegheny Lunatic\nAsylum in the aftermath of the Civil War where a severely wounded Union\nveteran, a 12-year-old girl and her mother, long abused by a Confederate\nsoldier, struggle to heal.\n\n![](/sites/default/files/20241024_dini_9866.jpg)\n\nJayne Anne Phillips accepts the 2024 Pulitzer Prize for Fiction from Columbia\nUniversity Interim President Katrina Armstrong. (David Dini/The Pulitzer\nPrizes)\n\n## Winning Work\n\n###\n\nNight Watch\n\nBy\n\nJayne Anne Phillips\n\n![](/cms/sites/default/files/content/9780451493330.jpeg)\n\n**LONGLISTED FOR THE NATIONAL BOOK AWARD IN FICTION \u2022 A  _NEW YORKER  _BEST\nBOOK OF THE YEAR \u2022 From one of our most accomplished novelists, a mesmerizing\nstory about a mother and daughter seeking refuge in the chaotic aftermath of\nthe Civil War\u2014and a brilliant portrait of family endurance against all odds  \n  \n\u201cA tour de force.\u201d \u2014Tayari Jones, author of _An American Marriage_**  \n  \nIn 1874, in the wake of the War, erasure, trauma, and namelessness haunt\ncivilians and veterans, renegades and wanderers, freedmen and runaways.\nTwelve-year-old ConaLee, the adult in her family for as long as she can\nremember, finds herself on a buckboard journey with her mother, Eliza, who\nhasn\u2019t spoken in more than a year. They arrive at the Trans-Allegheny Lunatic\nAsylum in West Virginia, delivered to the hospital\u2019s entrance by a war veteran\nwho has forced himself into their world. There, far from family, a beloved\nneighbor, and the mountain home they knew, they try to reclaim their lives.  \n  \nThe omnipresent vagaries of war and race rise to the surface as we learn their\nstory: their flight to the highest mountain ridges of western Virginia; the\ndisappearance of ConaLee\u2019s father, who left for the War and never returned.\nMeanwhile, in the asylum, they begin to find a new path. ConaLee pretends to\nbe her mother\u2019s maid; Eliza responds slowly to treatment. They get swept up in\nthe life of the facility\u2014the mysterious man they call the Night Watch; the\norphan child called Weed; the fearsome woman who runs the kitchen; the\nremarkable doctor at the head of the institution.  \n  \nEpic, enthralling, and meticulously crafted, _Night Watch  _is a stunning\nchronicle of surviving war and its aftermath.\n\n## Biography\n\n![](/sites/default/files/styles/bio_photo/public/bio-\nphoto/0000000023887_jayne_anne_phillips.jpg?itok=ENDdYxax)\n\n**Jayne Anne Phillips** is the author of _Black Tickets, Machine Dreams_ ,\n_Fast Lanes_ , _Shelter_ , _MotherKind_ , _Lark and Termite_ , _Quiet Dell_ ,\nand _Night Watch_. She is the recipient of a Guggenheim Fellowship, a Bunting\nFellowship, and two National Endowment for the Arts Fellowships. Winner of an\nArts and Letters Award and the Sue Kaufman Prize from the American Academy of\nArts and Letters, she was inducted into the Academy in 2018. A National Book\nAward finalist, and twice a finalist for the National Book Critics Circle\nAward, she lives in New York and Boston.\n\n## Finalists\n\n_Nominated as finalists in Fiction in 2024:_\n\n### [Ed Park](/finalists/ed-park)\n\nAn inventive postmodern novel that moves from the brutal Japanese occupation\nof the Korean Peninsula to a lonely Korean American boy\u2019s passion for the\nBuffalo Sabres, interlinked narratives that jump historical and imaginary time\nzones with humor, sorrow and irreverence.\n\n### [Yiyun Li](/finalists/yiyun-li)\n\nAn affecting volume of thematically and stylistically connected stories that\nare set around tasks carried out by caretakers of the infirm and mothers\nstruggling to carry on after the death of a child, work that mixes grief with\ngentle humor.\n\n## The Jury\n\n##  The Jury\n\n### Sam Sacks(Chair)\n\nBook Critic, The Wall Street Journal\n\n### [Michael Chabon*](/winners/michael-chabon)\n\nWriter, Berkeley, Calif.\n\n### Lan Samantha Chang\n\nProgram Director, Iowa Writers\u2019 Workshop; Elizabeth M.
