(1981)  \n  \n**James Larry Jameson III** is the 10th president of the [University of\nPennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania\n\"University of Pennsylvania\"), appointed in March 13, 2025, with a term\nextending through June 2027.[1] He initially assumed the role of Interim\nPresident in December 2023. Jameson also holds the title of Trustees\nUniversity Professor at Penn. Prior to his presidency, from 2011 to 2023,\nJameson served as Executive Vice President of the University of Pennsylvania\nfor the Health System and Dean of the Perelman School of Medicine, where he\nled significant advancements in medical education, research, and patient\ncare.[2]\n\n## Early life and education\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=J._Larry_Jameson&action=edit&section=1 \"Edit\nsection: Early life and education\")]\n\nJameson was born on [Fort Benning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Benning\n\"Fort Benning\") in [Columbus,\nGeorgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbus,_Georgia \"Columbus, Georgia\"),\nand raised in [Asheville, North\nCarolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asheville,_North_Carolina \"Asheville,\nNorth Carolina\").[3][4]\n\nFrom the [University of North Carolina at Chapel\nHill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_North_Carolina_at_Chapel_Hill\n\"University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill\"), Jameson received a [Bachelor\nof Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science \"Bachelor of\nScience\") with a major in chemistry with honors in 1976, a [Doctor of\nMedicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Medicine \"Doctor of\nMedicine\") in 1981, and a\n[Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy \"Doctor of\nPhilosophy\") in biochemistry in 1981.[5]\n\nJameson's doctoral dissertation was titled, _Regulation of microtubule\nassembly and steady-state dynamics_.[6] During his doctoral studies at UNC, he\nreceived the Merck Book Award in 1978, the [Alpha Omega\nAlpha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha_Omega_Alpha \"Alpha Omega Alpha\")\nmembership in 1980, and the Lange Book Award in 1980. In 1981, he received the\nIsaac Hall Manning Award and a Merit Award from the [UNC School of\nMedicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNC_School_of_Medicine \"UNC School of\nMedicine\").[7]\n\n## Career\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=J._Larry_Jameson&action=edit&section=2 \"Edit\nsection: Career\")]\n\nIn 1981, Jameson started as an intern and resident at [Massachusetts General\nHospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_General_Hospital\n\"Massachusetts General Hospital\") in\n[Boston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston \"Boston\"). In 1983, he became a\nresearch fellow at the [Howard Hughes Medical\nInstitute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Hughes_Medical_Institute\n\"Howard Hughes Medical Institute\")'s Laboratory of Molecular Endocrinology,\nstudying [transcriptional\nregulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcriptional_regulation\n\"Transcriptional regulation\") in endocrine genes with a focus on nuclear\nhormone receptors.[8][7]\n\nIn 1985, Jameson joined [Harvard Medical\nSchool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_Medical_School \"Harvard Medical\nSchool\") as an instructor in medicine, advancing to assistant professor in\n1987 and associate professor by 1992.[7] During this period, he served as\nchief of the Thyroid Unit at Massachusetts General Hospital from 1987 to 1993,\nwhere he co-directed training programs in endocrinology, diabetes, and\n[reproductive biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reproductive_biology\n\"Reproductive biology\"). His research included projects on [gene expression\nregulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene_expression_regulation \"Gene\nexpression regulation\") and [hormone\nreceptor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hormone_receptor \"Hormone receptor\")\ninteractions with pituitary genes, supported by grants from the [National\nInstitute of Diabetes and Digestive and Kidney\nDiseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_of_Diabetes_and_Digestive_and_Kidney_Diseases\n\"National Institute of Diabetes and Digestive and Kidney Diseases\").[7] In\n1990, his contributions were recognized with election to the [American Society\nfor Clinical\nInvestigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Society_for_Clinical_Investigation\n\"American Society for Clinical Investigation\").[8][7]\n\n### Northwestern University\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=J._Larry_Jameson&action=edit&section=3 \"Edit\nsection: Northwestern University\")]\n\nIn 1993, Jameson transitioned to [Northwestern\nUniversity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwestern_University\n\"Northwestern University\")'s [Feinberg School of\nMedicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feinberg_School_of_Medicine \"Feinberg\nSchool of Medicine\") as the Charles F. Kettering Professor of Medicine and\nchief of the Division of Endocrinology, Metabolism, and Molecular Medicine. He\ndirected Northwestern\u2019s Training Program in Endocrinology and Metabolism,\nfocusing on endocrine gene transcription, [thyroid\ndisease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thyroid_disease \"Thyroid disease\"), and\n[pituitary tumor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_tumor \"Pituitary\ntumor\") pathogenesis. He was elected a member of the [Association of American\nPhysicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_of_American_Physicians\n\"Association of American Physicians\") in 1995. That same year, he began\ncontributing as co-editor-in-chief of the textbook, _Endocrinology_ , a\nreference work on clinical and molecular endocrinology.[8][7]\n\nIn 2000, Jameson became chairman of the Department of Medicine at Northwestern\nUniversity, overseeing a broad range of research initiatives and patient care\nservices across multiple specialties. He directed the Hormone Action and\nCancer Group at the [Robert H. Lurie Comprehensive Cancer\nCenter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_H._Lurie_Comprehensive_Cancer_Center\n\"Robert H. Lurie Comprehensive Cancer Center\"), continuing his research into\nhormone receptor action and cancer biology. In 2004, Jameson was elected as a\nfellow of the [American Academy of Arts and\nSciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences\n\"American Academy of Arts and Sciences\") and became a [fellow of the American\nAssociation for the Advancement of\nScience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science\n\"Fellow of the American Association for the Advancement of Science\") in 2005.\nThe following year, he was inducted into the [National Academy of\nMedicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Medicine \"National\nAcademy of Medicine\").[8][7]\n\nIn 2007, Jameson was appointed dean of the Feinberg School of Medicine and\nvice president for medical affairs at Northwestern University. In these roles,\nhe expanded research initiatives and emphasized molecular medicine in\neducation. That same year, he also joined _[Harrison\u2019s Principles of Internal\nMedicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harrison's_Principles_of_Internal_Medicine\n\"Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine\")_ as an editor, contributing to\nits 15th through 21st editions and authoring sections on endocrinology,\ngenetics, and [sex\ndevelopment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_differentiation \"Sexual\ndifferentiation\").[7]\n\n### University of Pennsylvania\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=J._Larry_Jameson&action=edit&section=4 \"Edit\nsection: University of Pennsylvania\")]\n\nIn 2011, Jameson joined the [University of\nPennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania\n\"University of Pennsylvania\"), serving as the executive vice president for the\n[University of Pennsylvania Health\nSystem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania_Health_System\n\"University of Pennsylvania Health System\") and Dean of the [Perelman School\nof Medicine at the University of\nPennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perelman_School_of_Medicine_at_the_University_of_Pennsylvania\n\"Perelman School of Medicine at the University of Pennsylvania\"). His\nresponsibilities included expanding research and clinical infrastructure and\ndirecting strategic growth in medical education. His editorial roles further\nexpanded as he became editor-in-chief of _[The Journal of Clinical\nEndocrinology and\nMetabolism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Journal_of_Clinical_Endocrinology_and_Metabolism\n\"The Journal of Clinical Endocrinology and Metabolism\")_ from 2016 to 2021,\nand editor-in-chief of _Journal of the Endocrine Society_ from 2016 to 2019.\nSince 2013, Penn Medicine researchers launched at least 55 FDA approvals,\nincluding entire new therapeutic categories.[9]\n\nIn December 2023, Jameson was appointed interim president of the University of\nPennsylvania, following the resignation of [Liz\nMagill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liz_Magill \"Liz Magill\") amid her\n[congressional\nhearing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_United_States_Congress_hearing_on_antisemitism\n\"2023 United States Congress hearing on antisemitism\") controversies.[10] In\nMarch 2025, he was elevated to President to serve until 2027.[11][12][13]\n\nIn June 2026, Jameson announced that he would conclude his presidency as\nscheduled on June 30, 2027. Penn subsequently began a search for its 11th\npresident. [14]\n\n## Personal life\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=J._Larry_Jameson&action=edit&section=5 \"Edit\nsection: Personal life\")]\n\nJameson's paternal lineage traces back to his great-great-great grandfather,\nWilliam Jameson Sr., who emigrated from Ireland to [colonial\nAmerica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirteen_Colonies \"Thirteen Colonies\")\nin 1750.[3] William Jameson settled in [Easley, South\nCarolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Easley,_South_Carolina \"Easley, South\nCarolina\"), and served in the [American Revolutionary\nWar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War \"American\nRevolutionary War\").[3] For four subsequent generations, the family remained\nin [South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Carolina \"South\nCarolina\") until Jameson's father relocated to Asheville, North Carolina,\nwhere Jameson was raised.[3]\n\n## References\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=J._Larry_Jameson&action=edit&section=6 \"Edit\nsection: References\")]\n\n  1. \u2191 [\"A message from President J.
