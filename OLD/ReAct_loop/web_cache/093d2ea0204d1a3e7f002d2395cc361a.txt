Wedding People\")_ (2024)\n\n  \nWebsite| [alisonespach.com](http://alisonespach.com)  \n  \n**Alison Espach** (born September 26, 1984) is an American novelist. Espach is\nthe author of three novels\u2014 _The Adults_ (2011), _Notes on Your Sudden\nDisappearance_ (2022), and _[The Wedding\nPeople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wedding_People \"The Wedding\nPeople\")_ (2024). She is a professor of English at [Providence\nCollege](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Providence_College \"Providence\nCollege\") in Rhode Island.\n\n## Biography\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Alison_Espach&action=edit&section=1 \"Edit section:\nBiography\")]\n\nEspach was born in [Trumbull,\nConnecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trumbull,_Connecticut \"Trumbull,\nConnecticut\"), on September 26, 1984.[1][2] She is a 2007 graduate of\n[Providence College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Providence_College\n\"Providence College\") in\n[Providence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Providence,_Rhode_Island\n\"Providence, Rhode Island\"), Rhode Island, where English professor [Peter\nJohnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Johnson_\\(poet\\) \"Peter Johnson\n\\(poet\\)\") was a mentor.[3]\n\nAfter graduation, she pursued a [Master of Fine\nArts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Fine_Arts \"Master of Fine Arts\")\nat [Washington University in St.\nLouis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_University_in_St._Louis\n\"Washington University in St. Louis\"), Missouri, during which she wrote a\nthesis that would become her first novel, _The Adults_ (2011). She moved to\nNew York to teach writing after completing the program.[1] In 2013, she joined\nthe faculty of Providence College.[3] She is a professor of English.[4]\n\n## Novels\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Alison_Espach&action=edit&section=2 \"Edit section:\nNovels\")]\n\n### _The Adults_ (2011)\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Alison_Espach&action=edit&section=3 \"Edit section:\nThe Adults \\(2011\\)\")]\n\n_The Adults_ is a novel centered on a teenage girl growing up in a small,\nwealthy town in Connecticut.[5] It was published by [Charles Scribner's\nSons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Scribner's_Sons \"Charles\nScribner's Sons\") in early 2011.[6]\n\nIn the _[New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Times \"New\nYork Times\")_ , literary critic [Janet\nMaslin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janet_Maslin \"Janet Maslin\")'s review\ncharacterized the work as a \"[coming of\nage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coming_of_age_story \"Coming of age story\")\nwith a quick wit and a sharp eye\".[5] _[Kirkus\nReviews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirkus_Reviews \"Kirkus Reviews\")_ found\nthe work to be \"an enviable first effort.\"[7] [Dreamscape\nMedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dreamscape_Media \"Dreamscape Media\")\nproduced an audiobook, which was read by Tavia Gilbert; her narration was\nreceived positively by\n_[AudioFile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AudioFile_\\(magazine\\) \"AudioFile\n\\(magazine\\)\")_ magazine, with its reviewer writing that \"Subtle shifts of\ntone are all Gilbert needs to give Emily a convincing voice\".[8]\n\nFilm producers [Jamie Patricof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamie_Patricof\n\"Jamie Patricof\") and [Lynette\nHowell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lynette_Howell \"Lynette Howell\"), of\nElectric City Entertainment, [optioned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optioned\n\"Optioned\") the rights to the novel in 2012.[9]\n\n### _Notes on Your Sudden Disappearance_ (2022)\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Alison_Espach&action=edit&section=4 \"Edit section:\nNotes on Your Sudden Disappearance \\(2022\\)\")]\n\nEspach's second novel depicts a family following the death of its eldest\nchild. _[Kirkus Reviews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirkus_Reviews \"Kirkus\nReviews\")_ described it as a \"tragicomic bildungsroman in the shadow of\nloss\".[10]\n\n[Henry Holt and Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Holt_and_Company\n\"Henry Holt and Company\")'s hardcover design features a 2016 painting, \"The\nSwimming Pool\", by T. S. Harris from the stock photo website [Bridgeman\nImages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bridgeman_Images \"Bridgeman Images\"),\nwhile the paperback features a commercial stock photo of flowers behind the\ntypography.[11] [Macmillan\nAudio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macmillan_Audio \"Macmillan Audio\")\nreleased an audiobook adaptation narrated by Jesse Vilinsky.[12]\n\n### _The Wedding People_ (2024)\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Alison_Espach&action=edit&section=5 \"Edit section:\nThe Wedding People \\(2024\\)\")]\n\nMain article: [The Wedding\nPeople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wedding_People \"The Wedding People\")\n\nIn Espach's third novel, _[The Wedding\nPeople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wedding_People \"The Wedding\nPeople\")_ , Phoebe Stone travels to a hotel in [Newport, Rhode\nIsland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newport,_Rhode_Island \"Newport, Rhode\nIsland\") to enjoy one last night of self-indulgence before killing herself by\ndrug overdose. Her planned suicide is averted when she befriends the bride of\na wedding being held at the hotel. _The Wedding People_ appeared on the [_New\nYork Times_ Best Seller\nlist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times_Best_Seller_list \"The\nNew York Times Best Seller list\") and won the fiction category in the 2024\n[Goodreads Choice\nAwards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodreads_Choice_Awards \"Goodreads\nChoice Awards\").[13] It was shortlisted for the 'Published Novels' section of\nthe 2025 [Comedy Women in Print\nPrize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comedy_Women_in_Print_Prize \"Comedy Women\nin Print Prize\").[14] It is a finalist for the 2025 [Thurber Prize for\nAmerican Humor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thurber_Prize_for_American_Humor\n\"Thurber Prize for American Humor\").[15]\n\nPrior to its publication, in early 2024, [TriStar\nPictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TriStar_Pictures \"TriStar Pictures\")\nwas reported to have acquired rights to a film adaption of the novel, naming\n[Will Speck and Josh\nGordon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Will_Speck_and_Josh_Gordon \"Will Speck\nand Josh Gordon\") as directors and [Nicole\nHolofcener](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicole_Holofcener \"Nicole\nHolofcener\") as writer.[16] _[Chicago\nTribune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Tribune \"Chicago Tribune\")_\ncolumnist [John Warner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Warner_\\(writer\\)\n\"John Warner \\(writer\\)\") found _The Wedding People_ to be a \"highly\nrecommended, deeply satisfying read.\"[17] A _[Washington\nPost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Post \"Washington Post\")_ review\nof the audiobook adaption, narrated by Helen Laser, highlighted Laser's\nversatility in voicing the various characters.[18]\n\n## References\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Alison_Espach&action=edit&section=6 \"Edit section:\nReferences\")]\n\n  1. 1 2 Leo, Aaron (February 10, 2011).
