 \n  \n**Harvard Business School** (**HBS**) is the\n[graduate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graduate_school \"Graduate school\")\n[business school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_school \"Business\nschool\") of [Harvard\nUniversity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University \"Harvard\nUniversity\"), a [private](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_university\n\"Private university\") [Ivy League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivy_League\n\"Ivy League\") [research\nuniversity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_university \"Research\nuniversity\"). Located in [Allston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allston\n\"Allston\"), Massachusetts, HBS owns [Harvard Business\nPublishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_Business_Publishing \"Harvard\nBusiness Publishing\"), which publishes business books, leadership articles,\n[case studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Case_method \"Case method\"), and\n_[Harvard Business\nReview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_Business_Review \"Harvard\nBusiness Review\")_ , a monthly academic business magazine. It is also home to\nthe [Baker Library/Bloomberg\nCenter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baker_Library/Bloomberg_Center \"Baker\nLibrary/Bloomberg Center\"), the school's primary library. Harvard Business\nSchool is one of six [Ivy League business\nschools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Ivy_League_business_schools\n\"List of Ivy League business schools\"). HBS is part of the\n[M7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_M7_business_schools \"List of M7\nbusiness schools\") group of business schools recognized as having strong\n[MBA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Business_Administration \"Master\nof Business Administration\") programs,[2][3][4] that are often highly-ranked\nin US MBA rankings.[5]\n\n## History\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Harvard_Business_School&action=edit&section=1 \"Edit\nsection: History\")]\n\n[![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/55/Harvard_Business_School_%2854940852401%29.jpg/250px-\nHarvard_Business_School_%2854940852401%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Harvard_Business_School_\\(54940852401\\).jpg)[Baker\nLibrary/Bloomberg\nCenter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baker_Library/Bloomberg_Center \"Baker\nLibrary/Bloomberg Center\")\n\nThe school was established in 1908.[6] Initially established by the\n[humanities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanities \"Humanities\") faculty,\nit received independent status in 1910, and became a separate administrative\nunit in 1913. The first dean was historian [Edwin Francis\nGay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edwin_Francis_Gay \"Edwin Francis Gay\")\n(1867\u20131946).[7] Yogev (2001) explains the original concept:\n\n    This school of business and public administration was originally conceived as a school for diplomacy and government service on the model of the French _[Ecole des Sciences Politiques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sciences_Po \"Sciences Po\")._[8] The goal was an institution of higher learning that would offer a [Master of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts \"Master of Arts\") degree in the humanities field, with a major in business. In discussions about the curriculum, the suggestion was made to concentrate on specific business topics such as banking, railroads, and so on... Professor Lowell said the school would train qualified public administrators whom the government would have no choice but to employ, thereby building a better public administration... Harvard was blazing a new trail by educating young people for a career in business, just as its medical school trained doctors and its law faculty trained lawyers.[9]\n\nThe business school pioneered the development of the [case\nmethod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Case_method \"Case method\") of teaching,\ndrawing inspiration from this approach to legal education at Harvard.[10]\nCases are typically descriptions of real events in organizations. They are\nwritten by school faculty members who earn royalties on their sales.[11]\nStudents are positioned as managers and are presented with problems which they\nneed to analyze and provide recommendations on.[12]\n\nFrom its inception, the school enjoyed a close relationship with the corporate\nworld. Within a few years of its founding many business leaders were its\nalumni and were hiring other alumni for entry-level positions in their\nfirms.[13][14][15]\n\nAt its founding, the school accepted only male students. The [Training Course\nin Personnel Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard-\nRadcliffe_Program_in_Business_Administration \"Harvard-Radcliffe Program in\nBusiness Administration\"), founded at [Radcliffe\nCollege](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radcliffe_College \"Radcliffe College\")\nin 1937, was the beginning of business training for women at Harvard. HBS took\nover administration of that program from Radcliffe in 1954. In 1959, alumnae\nof the one-year program (by then known as the Harvard-Radcliffe Program in\nBusiness Administration) were permitted to apply to join the HBS MBA program\nas second-years. In December 1962, the faculty voted to allow women to enter\nthe MBA program directly. The first women to apply directly to the MBA program\nmatriculated in September 1963.[16]\n\nHarvard Business School played a role in the founding of the first business\nschoo"}
