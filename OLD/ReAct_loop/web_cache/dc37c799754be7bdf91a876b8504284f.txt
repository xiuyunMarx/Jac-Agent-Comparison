Jameson also holds the title of Trustees University Professor at Penn. Prior to his presidency, from 2011 to 2023, Jameson served as Executive Vice President of the University of Pennsylvania for the Health System and Dean of the Perelman School of Medicine, where he led significant advancements in medical education, research, and patient care.\n[\n2\n]\nEarly life and education\n[\nedit\n]\nJameson was born on\nFort Benning\nin\nColumbus, Georgia\n, and raised in\nAsheville, North Carolina\n.\n[\n3\n]\n[\n4\n]\nFrom the\nUniversity of North Carolina at Chapel Hill\n, Jameson received a\nBachelor of Science\nwith a major in chemistry with honors in 1976, a\nDoctor of Medicine\nin 1981, and a\nPh.D.\nin biochemistry in 1981.\n[\n5\n]\nJameson's doctoral dissertation was titled,\nRegulation of microtubule assembly and steady-state dynamics\n.\n[\n6\n]\nDuring his doctoral studies at UNC, he received the Merck Book Award in 1978, the\nAlpha Omega Alpha\nmembership in 1980, and the Lange Book Award in 1980. In 1981, he received the Isaac Hall Manning Award and a Merit Award from the\nUNC School of Medicine\n.\n[\n7\n]\nCareer\n[\nedit\n]\nIn 1981, Jameson started as an intern and resident at\nMassachusetts General Hospital\nin\nBoston\n. In 1983, he became a research fellow at the\nHoward Hughes Medical Institute\n's Laboratory of Molecular Endocrinology, studying\ntranscriptional regulation\nin endocrine genes with a focus on nuclear hormone receptors.\n[\n8\n]\n[\n7\n]\nIn 1985, Jameson joined\nHarvard Medical School\nas an instructor in medicine, advancing to assistant professor in 1987 and associate professor by 1992.\n[\n7\n]\nDuring this period, he served as chief of the Thyroid Unit at Massachusetts General Hospital from 1987 to 1993, where he co-directed training programs in endocrinology, diabetes, and\nreproductive biology\n. His research included projects on\ngene expression regulation\nand\nhormone receptor\ninteractions with pituitary genes, supported by grants from the\nNational Institute of Diabetes and Digestive and Kidney Diseases\n.\n[\n7\n]\nIn 1990, his contributions were recognized with election to the\nAmerican Society for Clinical Investigation\n.\n[\n8\n]\n[\n7\n]\nNorthwestern University\n[\nedit\n]\nIn 1993, Jameson transitioned to\nNorthwestern University\n's\nFeinberg School of Medicine\nas the Charles F. Kettering Professor of Medicine and chief of the Division of Endocrinology, Metabolism, and Molecular Medicine. He directed Northwestern\u2019s Training Program in Endocrinology and Metabolism, focusing on endocrine gene transcription,\nthyroid disease\n, and\npituitary tumor\npathogenesis. He was elected a member of the\nAssociation of American Physicians\nin 1995. That same year, he began contributing as co-editor-in-chief of the textbook,\nEndocrinology\n, a reference work on clinical and molecular endocrinology.\n[\n8\n]\n[\n7\n]\nIn 2000, Jameson became chairman of the Department of Medicine at Northwestern University, overseeing a broad range of research initiatives and patient care services across multiple specialties. He directed the Hormone Action and Cancer Group at the\nRobert H. Lurie Comprehensive Cancer Center\n, continuing his research into hormone receptor action and cancer biology. In 2004, Jameson was elected as a fellow of the\nAmerican Academy of Arts and Sciences\nand became a\nfellow of the American Association for the Advancement of Science\nin 2005. The following year, he was inducted into the\nNational Academy of Medicine\n.\n[\n8\n]\n[\n7\n]\nIn 2007, Jameson was appointed dean of the Feinberg School of Medicine and vice president for medical affairs at Northwestern University. In these roles, he expanded research initiatives and emphasized molecular medicine in education. That same year, he also joined\nHarrison\u2019s Principles of Internal Medicine\nas an editor, contributing to its 15th through 21st editions and authoring sections on endocrinology, genetics, and\nsex development\n.\n[\n7\n]\nUniversity of Pennsylvania\n[\nedit\n]\nIn 2011, Jameson joined the\nUniversity of Pennsylvania\n, serving as the executive vice president for the\nUniversity of Pennsylvania Health System\nand Dean of the\nPerelman School of Medicine at the University of Pennsylvania\n. His responsibilities included expanding research and clinical infrastructure and directing strategic growth in medical education. His editorial roles further expanded as he became editor-in-chief of\nThe Journal of Clinical Endocrinology and Metabolism\nfrom 2016 to 2021, and editor-in-chief of\nJournal of the Endocrine Society\nfrom 2016 to 2019. Since 2013, Penn Medicine researchers launched at least 55 FDA approvals, including entire new therapeutic categories.\n[\n9\n]\nIn December 2023, Jameson was appointed interim president of the University of Pennsylvania, following the resignation of\nLiz Magill\namid her\ncongressional hearing\ncontroversies.\n[\n10\n]\nIn March 2025, he was elevated to President to serve until 2027.\n[\n11\n]\n[\n12\n]\n[\n13\n]\nIn June 2026, Jameson announced that he would conclude his presidency as scheduled on June 30, 2027. Penn subsequently began a search for its 11th president.\n[\n14\n]\nPersonal life\n[\nedit\n]\nJameson's paternal lineage traces back to his great-great-great grandfather, William Jameson Sr., who emigrated from Ireland to\ncolonial America\nin 1750.\n[\n3\n]\nWilliam Jameson settled in\nEasley, South Carolina\n, and served in the\nAmerican Revolutionary War\n.\n[\n3\n]\nFor four subsequent generations, the family remained in\nSouth Carolina\nuntil Jameson's father relocated to Asheville, North Carolina, where Jameson was raised.\n[\n3\n]\nReferences\n[\nedit\n]\n\u2191\n\"A message from President J.
