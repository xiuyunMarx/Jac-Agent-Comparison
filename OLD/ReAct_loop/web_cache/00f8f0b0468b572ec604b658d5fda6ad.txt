 \n  \n**Yonsei University** ([Korean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_language\n\"Korean language\"): \uc5f0\uc138\ub300\ud559\uad50; [Hanja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanja\n\"Hanja\"): \u5ef6\u4e16\u5927\u5b78\u6821) is a\n[private](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_university \"Private\nuniversity\") [Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_university\n\"Christian university\") [research\nuniversity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_university \"Research\nuniversity\") in [Seoul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul \"Seoul\"), [South\nKorea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Korea \"South Korea\"). Yonsei\nUniversity is one of the three most prestigious universities in the country,\npart of a group referred to as [SKY\nuniversities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SKY_\\(universities\\) \"SKY\n\\(universities\\)\").[3]\n\nYonsei University traces its roots to the first modern medical center in\nKorea, _Gwanghyewon_ ([Korean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_language\n\"Korean language\"): \uad11\ud61c\uc6d0; [Hanja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanja \"Hanja\"):\n\u5ee3\u60e0\u9662; lit. 'House of Extended Grace') founded in April 1885, now [Severance\nUnion Medical College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Severance_Hospital\n\"Severance Hospital\"). The institution in its current university form was\nestablished in January 1957 through the union of Yonhi College (\uc5f0\ud76c\uc804\ubb38\ud559\uad50;\n\u5ef6\u79a7\u5c08\u9580\u5b78\u6821) and Severance.[4] As a tribute, the name \"Yonsei\" was derived from the\nfirst syllables of the names of its two parent institutions, \"Yon; \uc5f0; \u5ef6\" from\nYonhi College and \"Sei; \uc138; \u4e16\" from Severance Union Medical College. Yonhi\nCollege was one of the first modern colleges, founded as Chosun Christian\nCollege (\uc870\uc120\uae30\ub3c5\uad50\ub300\ud559; \u671d\u9bae\u57fa\u7763\u6559\u5927\u5b78) in March 1915. The union was a result of a lasting\nbilateral cooperation between the colleges that began in the 1920s. The\ninstitutions were the first of their kinds in Korea.\n\nThe student body consists of 18,200 undergraduate students, 11,632 graduate\nstudents, 4,518 faculty members, 6,788 staff, and 257,931 alumni.[_[citation\nneeded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed\n\"Wikipedia:Citation needed\")_] Yonsei University operates its main campus in\nSeoul and offers graduate, postgraduate, and doctoral programs in Korean and\nEnglish.\n\n## History\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Yonsei_University&action=edit&section=1 \"Edit\nsection: History\")]\n\n### Beginnings (1885\u20131916)\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Yonsei_University&action=edit&section=2 \"Edit\nsection: Beginnings \\(1885\u20131916\\)\")]\n\n[![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2f/Gwanghyewon_Main_Door.jpg/250px-\nGwanghyewon_Main_Door.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Gwanghyewon_Main_Door.jpg)\nGwanghyewon's main door and engravings\n\nThe Yonsei University Medical School dates to April 10, 1885, when the first\nmodern hospital to practice [Western\nmedicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_medicine \"Western medicine\")\nin Korea, _Gwanghyewon_ , was established.[5] The hospital was founded by\n[Horace Newton Allen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horace_Newton_Allen\n\"Horace Newton Allen\"), the\n[American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States \"United States\")\n[Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant \"Protestant\")\n[missionary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missionary \"Missionary\") appointed\nto Korea by the [Presbyterian Church in the\nUSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presbyterian_Church_in_the_United_States_of_America\n\"Presbyterian Church in the United States of America\"). The hospital was\nrenamed [Chejungwon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chejungwon \"Chejungwon\") on\nApril 26.[6] As there appeared difficulties, the church appointed\n[Canadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian \"Canadian\") [Oliver R.\nAvison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_R._Avison \"Oliver R. Avison\") to\nrun _Chejungwon_ on July 16, 1893. _Gwanghyewon_ was financed at first by the\nKorean government, while the medical staff was provided by the church.\nHowever, by 1894 when the [First Sino-Japanese\nWar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Sino-Japanese_War \"First Sino-\nJapanese War\") and [Gabo reforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabo_reforms\n\"Gabo reforms\") took place, the government was not able to continue its\nfinancial support, thus management of _Chejungwon_ came fully under the\nchurch. In 1899, Avison returned to the US and attended a conference of\nmissionaries in New York City where he elaborated on the medical project in\nKorea. [Louis Severance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Severance \"Louis\nSeverance\"), a businessman and philanthropist from [Cleveland,\nOhio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland,_Ohio \"Cleveland, Ohio\"), was\npresent and was deeply moved. He later paid for the major portion of the\nconstruction costs of new buildings for the medical facility. _Chejungwon_ was\nrenamed [Severance Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Severance_Hospital\n\"Severance Hospital\") after him.[7]\n\n_Chejungwon_ (later Severance Hospital) was primarily a hospital, but it also\nperformed medical education as an attachment. The hospital admitted its first\nclass of 16 medical students selected through examinations in 1886, one year\nafter its establishment.[8] By 1899, Chejungwon Medical School was\nindependently recognized. Following the increase of diversity in missionary\ndenominations in Korea, collaboration began to form. _Chejungwon_ began to\nreceive medical staff, school faculty, and financial support from the Union\nCouncil of Korean Missionaries (\ud55c\uad6d\uc5f0\ud569\uc120\uad50\ud611\uc758\ud68c; \u97d3\u570b\u806f\u5408\u5ba3\u654e\u5354\u8b70\u6703) in 1912. Accordingly,\nthe medical school was renamed as Severance Union Medical College in 1913.[9]\n\nThe rest of Yonsei University traces its origins to Chosun Christian College,\nwhich was founded on March 5, 1915, by an American Protestant missionary,\n[Horace Grant Underwood](https://e"}
