Pao\n(Big Red Robe, \u5927\u7ea2\u888d) is a\nWuyi rock tea\ngrown in the\nWuyi Mountains\nof\nFujian Province\n, China.\n[\n1\n]\nDa Hong Pao has a unique orchid fragrance and a long-lasting sweet aftertaste.\n[\n2\n]\nDry Da Hong Pao has a shape like tightly knotted ropes or slightly twisted strips, and is green and brown in color. After brewing, the tea is orange-yellow, bright, and clear.\n[\n2\n]\nDa Hong Pao can retain its flavor for nine steepings.\n[\n3\n]\nThe tea is often known to be extremely expensive.\n[\n4\n]\n[\n5\n]\nBrew\n[\nedit\n]\nA traditional way to brew Da Hong Pao is by using a\nPurple Clay Teapot\nand\n100\n\u00b0C (212\n\u00b0F)\nwater.\n[\n3\n]\nPurified water is considered by some to be the best choice to brew Da Hong Pao. The boiled water should be used immediately after boiling. Boiling the water for a long time or storing it for a long time after boiling will influence the taste of the Da Hong Pao.\n[\n6\n]\nThe third and fourth steeping are considered by some to have the best taste.\n[\n6\n]\nProduction\n[\nedit\n]\nThere are seven steps needed to make Da Hong Pao.\n[\n6\n]\n\u91c7\u6458\n(picking): Da Hong Pao is picked once a year, generally in May and June. The standard for fresh leaf picking is 1 bud with 2\u20143 leaves.\n[\n1\n]\n\u840e\u51cb\n(withering): The picked tea leaves are spread evenly in the sun, using sunlight and breeze to evaporate the water.\n[\n6\n]\n[\n7\n]\n\u51c9\u9752\n(cooling): After withering, the tea leaves are moved inside and cooled down.\n[\n6\n]\n\u505a\u9752\n(making): The tea leaves are shaken and rolled in a big bamboo sieve. This may be conducive to the oxidation of polyphenols inside the tea leaves.\n[\n1\n]\nThis step is the most crucial part to form the fragrance, taste, and rhyme of Da Hong Pao.\n[\n1\n]\n[\n6\n]\nAnd it is also the most complicated and time-consuming step.\n[\n7\n]\n\u7092\u9752\n(stir-fry): There are two types of stir-fry, hand stir-fry and mechanical stir-fry leaves. The pot temperature of hand stir-fry is between\n140\u2013160\n\u00b0C (284\u2013320\n\u00b0F)\n. People put on gloves and keep turning over the tea leaves with hands. The pot temperature of mechanical stir-fry is between\n220\u2013260\n\u00b0C (428\u2013500\n\u00b0F)\n, no hands needed.\n[\n6\n]\n\u63c9\u637b\n(kneading): There are two types of kneading, hand kneading and mechanical kneading. By kneading, rolling, and twisting, the tea leaves will form a cord shape.\n[\n6\n]\n\u70d8\u57f9\n(baking): This step includes putting the tea in a big basket and using different temperatures to heat and dry the leaves, which usually takes hours.\n[\n6\n]\nPrice and value\n[\nedit\n]\nThe best Da Hong Pao are from the mother Da Hong Pao tea trees. Mother Da Hong Pao tea trees have thousands of years of history. There are only 6 mother trees remaining on the stiff cliff of Jiulongyu (\nWuyi Mountains\n), which is considered a rare treasure.\n[\n1\n]\nBecause of its scarcity and superior tea quality, Da Hong Pao is known as the \"King of Tea\".\n[\n8\n]\nIn 2006, the Wuyi city government insured these 6 mother trees with a value of 100 million RMB.\n[\n9\n]\nIn the same year, the Wuyi city government also decided to prohibit anyone from privately collecting teas from the mother Da Hong Pao tea trees.\n[\n10\n]\nOne of the last batch of Da Hong Pao harvested and made from the mother trees has been collected in the Palace Museum in Beijing.\n[\n10\n]\n20g of Da Hong Pao tea from one of the mother plants was sold for \u00a5208,000 in 2005. It is the highest auction record for Da Hong Pao.\n[\n6\n]\nSamples of Da Hong Pao\nThe majority of Da Hong Pao on the market now are artificially-bred, which maintained the characteristics of the Da Hong Pao.\n[\n1\n]\n[\n10\n]\nIt is much cheaper than Da Hong Pao from the mother tea trees. The price depends heavily on its quality.\n[\n6\n]\nType of t"}
