work during the 2023 calendar year on May 6, 2024.\n[\n1\n]\nPrizes\n[\nedit\n]\nWinners and finalists for the prizes are listed below, with the winners marked in\nbold\n.\n[\n2\n]\nJournalism\n[\nedit\n]\nPublic Service\nProPublica\n, for the work of Joshua Kaplan, Justin Elliott,\nBrett Murphy\n, Alex Mierjeski and Kirsten Berg, for \"groundbreaking and ambitious reporting that pierced the thick wall of secrecy surrounding the\nSupreme Court\nto reveal how a small group of politically influential billionaires wooed justices with lavish gifts and travel, pushing the Court to adopt its first code of conduct.\"\nKFF Health News\nand\nCox Media Group\n, \"for uncovering millions of cases in which the Social Security Administration overpaid beneficiaries, then demanded immediate repayment \u2014 imposing debts on elderly and disabled people who had already spent the funds.\"\nThe Washington Post\n, \"for its sobering examination of the AR-15 semi-automatic rifle, which forced readers to reckon with the horrors wrought by the weapon often used for mass shootings in America.\"\nBreaking News Reporting\nStaff of\nLookout Santa Cruz\n, California\n, \"for its detailed and nimble community-focused coverage, over a holiday weekend, of catastrophic flooding and mudslides that displaced thousands of residents and destroyed more than 1,000 homes and businesses.\"\nStaff of the\nHonolulu Civil Beat\n, \"for its distinctive, sweeping and urgent coverage of\nthe Maui wildfires\nthat killed more than 100 people and left a historic town in ruins, reporting that held officials to account and chronicled the aftermath and efforts to rebuild.\"\nStaff of the\nLos Angeles Times\n, \"for urgent and thoughtful coverage of a Lunar New Year\novernight shooting\nthat left 11 senior citizens dead, demonstrating clear knowledge of and commitment to the local Asian communities.\"\nInvestigative Reporting\nHannah Dreier\nof\nThe New York Times\n, \"for a deeply reported series of stories revealing the stunning reach of migrant child labor across the United States\u2014and the corporate and governmental failures that perpetuate it.\"\nCasey Ross and Bob Herman of\nSTAT\n, \"for exposing how\nUnitedHealth Group\n, the nation's largest health insurer, used an unregulated algorithm to override clinicians' judgments and deny care, highlighting the dangers of AI use in medicine.\"\nStaff of\nBloomberg\n, \"for a deep and rigorous investigation of how the US government aided the global spread of gun violence, prompting the\nBiden\nadministration to halt most gun exports for 90 days while it reviewed the federal government's marketing relationship with gun manufacturers.\"\nExplanatory Reporting\nSarah Stillman\nof\nThe New Yorker\n, \"for a searing indictment of our legal system\u2019s reliance on the felony murder charge and its disparate consequences, often devastating for communities of color.\"\nStaff of\nBloomberg\n, \"for rigorous, far-reaching reporting that holds corporate water profiteers to account and exposes how they willfully exacerbate the effects of climate change at the expense of less powerful communities.\"\nStaffs of\nThe Texas Tribune\n,\nProPublica\n, and\nFRONTLINE\n, \"for advancing understanding of law enforcement\u2019s catastrophic response to\nthe mass shooting at a Uvalde\n, Texas elementary school and also for documenting the political and policy shortcomings that have led to similar deadly police failures across the country.\"\nLocal Reporting\nSarah Conway\nof\nCity Bureau\nand Trina Reynolds-Tyler of the\nInvisible Institute\n, \"for their investigative series on missing Black girls and women in Chicago that revealed how systemic racism and police department neglect contributed to the crisis.\"\nJerry Mitchell, Ilyssa Daly, Brian Howey and Nate Rosenfield of\nMississippi Today\nand\nThe New York Times\n, \"for their detailed examination of corruption and abuse, including the torturing of suspects, by Mississippi sheriffs and their officers over two decades.\"\nStaff of\nThe Villages Daily Sun\n, \"for its comprehensive investigation and moment-by-moment account of Florida officials\u2019 inaction before, during and after\nHurricane Ian\n, the deadliest storm to strike the state since 1935.\"\nNational Reporting\nStaff of\nReuters\n, \"for an eye-opening series of accountability stories focused on\nElon Musk\n\u2019s automobile and aerospace businesses, stories that displayed remarkable breadth and depth and provoked official probes of his companies\u2019 practices in Europe and the United States.\"\nStaff of\nThe Washington Post\n, \"for its sobering examination of the AR-15 semi-automatic rifle, which forced readers to reckon with the horrors wrought by the weapon often used for mass shootings in America.\"\nBianca V\u00e1zquez Toness and Sharon Lurye of the\nAssociated Press\n, \"for a deeply reported series on the corrosive effect of the pandemic on public education, highlighting the staggering number of students missing from classrooms across America.\"\nDave Philipps of\nThe New York Times\n, \"for groundbreaking reporting that uncovered a pattern of traumatic brain injuries among U.S. troops from blast exposures caused by the weapons they were firing.\"\nInternational Reporting\nStaff of\nThe New York Times\n, \"for its wide-ranging and revelatory coverage of\nHamas\u2019 lethal attack\nin southern Israel on October 7, Israel's intelligence failures and the Israeli military's sweeping, deadly response in Gaza.\"\nJulie Turkewitz and Federico Rios of\nThe New York Times\n, \"for their immersive and ambitious coverage of \"migration purgatory\" in the Dari\u00e9n Gap between Colombia and Panama.\"\nStaff of\nThe Washington Post\n, \"for a sweeping on-the-ground investigation in India that exposed the methodical undermining of the world\u2019s largest democracy by\nNarendra Modi\nand his Hindu nationalist allies, who have deployed social media to foment hate and pressure American tech giants to bend to government power.\"\nFeature Writing\nKatie Engelhart\n, contributing writer,\nThe New York Times\n, \"for her fair-minded portrait of a family\u2019s legal and emotional struggles during a matriarch\u2019s progressive dementia that sensitively probes the mystery of a person\u2019s essential self.\"\nJennifer Senior of\nThe Atlantic\n, \"for her exquisitely rendered account of her disabled aunt, who was institutionalized as a small child, and the lasting effects on her family, told in the context of present-day care and intervention that make different outcomes possible.\"\nKeri Blakinger\nof\nThe Marshall Project\n, \"for her insightful, humane portrait, reported with great difficulty, of men on Death Row in Texas who play clandestine games of \u201cDungeons & Dragons,\u201d countering their extreme isolation with elaborate fantasy. (Co-published with\nThe New York Times Magazine\n.)\"\nCommentary\nVladimir Kara-Murza\n, contributor,\nThe Washington Post\n, \"for passionate columns written under great personal risk from his prison cell, warning of the consequences of dissent in\nVladimir Putin\n's Russia and insisting on a democratic future for his country.\"\nBrian Lyman of the\nAlabama Reflector\n, \"for brave, clear and pointed columns that challenge ever-more-repressive state policies flouting democratic norms and targeting vulnerable populations, written with the command and authority of a veteran political observer.\"\nJay Caspian Kang\nof\nThe New Yorker\n, \"for original columns that force us to reexamine popular narratives and reframe such critical topics as affirmative action, racial politics and the portrayal of gun violence.\"\nCriticism\nJustin Chang\nof the\nLos Angeles Times\n, \"for richly evocative and genre-spanning film criticism that reflects on the contemporary moviegoing experience.\"\nVinson Cunningham\nof\nThe New Yorker\n, \"for theater reviews that reflect a formidable knowledge of the stage and the mechanics of performance along with canny observations on the human condition.\"\nZadie Smith\n, contributor,\nThe New York Review of Books\n, \"for a review of the film \u201c\nT\u00e1r\n\u201d that addressed with wit and ease such consequential themes as mortality and the clash of generations.\"\nEditorial Writing\nDavid E. Hoffman\nof\nThe Washington Post\n, \"for a compelling and well-researched series on new technologies and the tactics authoritarian regimes use to repress dissent in the digital age, and how they can be fought.\"\nBrandon McGinley and Rebecca Spiess of the\nPittsburgh Post-Gazette\n, \"for ambitious, investigative editorials that examine a collapse in services for the homeless in Pittsburgh, and the city\u2019s failure to account for millions of dollars meant to offer relief.\"\nIsadora Rangel of the\nMiami Herald\n, \"for a scathing series that roots the city\u2019s multiple political scandals in a troubled local democracy and champions electoral reforms.\"\nIllustrated Reporting and Commentary\nMedar de la Cruz, contributor,\nThe New Yorker\n, \"for his visually-driven story set inside\nRikers Island jail\nusing bold black-and-white images that humanize the prisoners and staff through their hunger for books.\"\nAngie Wang, contributor,\nThe New Yorker\n, \"for a vivid illustrated journey with her toddler that explains how human language learning can never be supplanted by AI.\"\nClaire Healy, Nicole Dungca and Ren Galeno, contributor, of\nThe Washington Post\n, \"for masterful and sensitive use of the comic form to reveal the story of a great injustice to a group of Filipinos exhibited at the 1904 World\u2019s Fair in St. Louis, where some of them died.\"\nClay Bennett of the\nChattanooga Times Free Press\n, \"for a portfolio of deceptively gentle, mostly wordless cartoons full of juxtapositions that ably communicate complex, sophisticated messages.\"\nBreaking News Photography\nPhotography Staff of\nReuters\n, \"for raw and urgent photographs documenting the October 7th deadly attack in Israel by Hamas and the first weeks of Israel\u2019s devastating assault on Gaza.\"\nAdem Altan of\nAgence France-Presse\n, \"for a heartbreaking image of a man clutching the hand of his deceased daughter a day after\na 7.8-magnitude earthquake\nkilled more than 50,000 people in Turkey and Syria.\"\nNicole S. Hester of\nThe Tennessean\n, \"for a distressing image of a young girl looking out of a school bus in anguish as she is evacuated from the scene of\na deadly shooting\nat The Covenant School in Nashville.\"\nFeature Photography\nPhotography Staff of\nAssociated Press\n, \"for poignant photographs chronicling unprecedented masses of migrants and their arduous journey north from Colombia to the border of the United States.\"\nHannah Reyes Morales, contributor,\nThe New York Times\n, \"for a creative series of photographs documenting a \u201cyouthquake\u201d occurring in Africa where, by 2050, the continent will account for one-quarter of the world's population and one-third of its young people.\"\nNanna Heitmann, contributor,\nThe New York Times\n, \"for illuminating photographs portraying a generation living under President Vladimir Putin\u2019s resurgent nationalism while\nRussia is at war in Ukraine\n.\"\nAudio Reporting\nStaffs of the\nInvisible Institute\nand\nUSG Audio\n, \"for a powerful series that revisits a Chicago hate crime from the 1990s, a fluid amalgam of memoir, community history and journalism.\"\nDan Slepian\nand Preeti Varathan of\nNBC News\n, \"for their relentless 20-year investigation that resulted in a wrongfully-convicted man finally receiving clemency.\"\nLauren Chooljian, Alison Macadam, Jason Moon, Daniel Barrick and Katie Colaneri of\nNew Hampshire Public Radio\n, \"for their gripping and extensively reported investigation of corruption and sexual abuse within the lucrative recovery industry that sought accountability despite
