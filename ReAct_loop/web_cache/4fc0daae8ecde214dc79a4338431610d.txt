 _[Regio VIII Forum\nRomanum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regio_VIII_Forum_Romanum \"Regio VIII\nForum Romanum\")_ [](http://wikimapia.org/20941/Wikimapia)  \nHistory  \nBuilt| 44\u201329 BC  \nBuilt by| [Julius Caesar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julius_Caesar \"Julius\nCaesar\")  \n  \nThe **Curia Julia** (Latin: _Curia Iulia_) is the third named\n_[curia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curia \"Curia\")_ , or [senate\nhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate \"Senate\"), in the\n[ancient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome \"Ancient Rome\") city of\n[Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome \"Rome\"). It was built in 44 BC, when\n[Julius Caesar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julius_Caesar \"Julius Caesar\")\nreplaced [Faustus Cornelius\nSulla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faustus_Cornelius_Sulla_\\(quaestor_54_BC\\)\n\"Faustus Cornelius Sulla \\(quaestor 54 BC\\)\")'s reconstructed [Curia\nCornelia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curia_Cornelia \"Curia Cornelia\"),\nwhich had replaced the [Curia\nHostilia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curia_Hostilia \"Curia Hostilia\").\nCaesar did so to redesign both spaces within the\n[Comitium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comitium \"Comitium\") and the [Roman\nForum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Forum \"Roman Forum\"). The\nalterations within the Comitium reduced the prominence of the\n[Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Senate \"Roman Senate\") and\ncleared the original space.[1]\n\nThe work was interrupted by Caesar's assassination at the [Curia of\nPompey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curia_of_Pompey \"Curia of Pompey\") of\nthe [Theatre of Pompey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theatre_of_Pompey\n\"Theatre of Pompey\"), where the [Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate\n\"Senate\") had been meeting temporarily while the work was completed. The\nproject was eventually finished by Caesar's heir and successor, [Augustus\nCaesar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustus \"Augustus\"), in 29 BC.[2]\n\nThe Curia Julia is one of a handful of Roman structures that survive mostly\nintact. This is due to its conversion into the basilica of [Sant'Adriano al\nForo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sant'Adriano_al_Foro \"Sant'Adriano al\nForo\") in the 7th century and several later restorations. The roof, the upper\nelevations of the side walls and the rear\n[fa\u00e7ade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fa\u00e7ade \"Fa\u00e7ade\") are modern and date\nfrom the remodeling of the\n[deconsecrated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deconsecrated \"Deconsecrated\")\nchurch, in the 1930s.\n\n## History\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Curia_Julia&action=edit&section=1 \"Edit section:\nHistory\")]\n\n[![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f1/Curia_iulia_02.JPG/250px-\nCuria_iulia_02.JPG?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Curia_iulia_02.JPG)The\nCuria Julia in the [Roman Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Forum\n\"Roman Forum\"), the seat of the imperial Senate\n\nThere were many curiae during the history of the Roman civilization, many of\nthem existing at the same time. Curia means simply \"meeting house\". While the\nsenate met regularly at the curia within the\n[comitium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comitium \"Comitium\") space, there\nwere many other structures designed for it to meet when the need occurred: for\nexample, meeting with someone who was not allowed to enter the\n[sanctified](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/sanctified \"wikt:sanctified\")\ncuriae of the Senate.\n\nThe Curia Julia is the third named curia within the comitium. Each structure\nwas rebuilt a number of times but originated from a single [Etruscan\ntemple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etruscan_temple \"Etruscan temple\"),\nbuilt to honor the truce of the Sabine conflict. When this original temple was\ndestroyed, [Tullus Hostilius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tullus_Hostilius\n\"Tullus Hostilius\") rebuilt it and gave it his name. It lasted for a few\nhundred years until the curia was destroyed by fire from the impromptu funeral\nof [Publius Clodius\nPulcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Publius_Clodius_Pulcher \"Publius\nClodius Pulcher\"). A new structure was dedicated to its financial benefactor,\n[Faustus Cornelius\nSulla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faustus_Cornelius_Sulla_\\(quaestor_54_BC\\)\n\"Faustus Cornelius Sulla \\(quaestor 54 BC\\)\").\n\nThe structure now in the Forum is the second incarnation of Caesar's curia. In\n94, the Curia Julia was restored under\n[Domitian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domitian \"Domitian\").[3][4] In 283,\nit was heavily damaged by a fire, at the time of Emperor\n[Carinus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carinus \"Carinus\").[5] From 284 to\n305, the Curia was then rebuilt by\n[Diocletian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocletian \"Diocletian\"). It is the\nremnants of Diocletian's building that stands today. In 412, the Curia was\nrestored again, this time by [Urban\nPrefect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Praetorian_prefect \"Praetorian\nprefect\") [Annius Eucharius\nEpiphanius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annius_Eucharius_Epiphanius \"Annius\nEucharius Epiphanius\").\n\nIn July 1923, the Italian government acquired the Curia Julia and the adjacent\nconvent of the Church of S. Adriano from the [Collegio di\nSpagna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collegio_di_Spagna \"Collegio di Spagna\")\nfor approximately \u00a316,000.[6]\n\n## Description\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Curia_Julia&action=edit&section=2 \"Edit section:\nDescription\")]\n\n[![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d3/Denario_di_ottaviano_con_la_curia_iulia.jpg/250px-\nDenario_di_ottaviano_con_la_curia_iulia.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Denario_di_ottaviano_con_la_curia_iulia.jpg)A\ndenarius of Octavian showing the front of the Curia Julia\n[![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/00/Curia_Iulia_front.jpg/250px-\nCuria_Iulia_front.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Curia_Iulia_front.jpg)The\nfront of the Curia Julia\n[![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/06/Victoriastatue.jpg/250px-\nVictoriastatue.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Victoriastatue.jpg)The\nstatue of Altar of\n[Victory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_\\(mythology\\) \"Victoria\n\\(mythology\\)\") on a coin issued under Augustus, matching its description by\n[Prudentius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prudentius \"Prudentius\").[7]\n[![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/df/Ancient_Roman_Senate_Bronze_doors.jpg/250px-\nAncient_Roman_Senate_Bronze_doors.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Ancient_Roman_Senate_Bronze_doors.jpg)The\nancient bronze doors of the Curia Julia, now in the [Archbasilica of Saint\nJohn Lateran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archbasilica_of_Saint_John_Lateran\n\"Archbasilica of Saint John Lateran\")\n\nThe exterior of the Curia Julia features\n[brick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brick \"Brick\")-faced\n[concrete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concrete \"Concrete\") with a huge\nbuttress at each angle. The lower part of the front wall was decorated with\nslabs of [marble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marble \"Marble\"). The upper\npart was covered with [stucco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stucco \"Stucco\")\nimitation of white marble blocks. A single flight of steps leads up to the\n[bronze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze \"Bronze\") doors. The current\nbronze doors are modern replicas; the original bronze doors were transferred\nto the [Basilica of St. John\nLateran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_of_St._John_Lateran \"Basilica\nof St. John Lateran\") by [Pope Alexander\nVII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Alexander_VII \"Pope Alexander VII\") in\n1660.[8]\n\nA coin was found within the doors during their transfer.[9] That allowed\narchaeologists to date repairs made to the Senate House and the addition of\nthe bronze doors to the reign of Emperor\n[Domitian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domitian \"Domitian\") (AD 81\u201396). The\noriginal appearance of the Senate House is known from an Emperor Augustus\nDenarius of 28 BC, which shows the veranda held up by columns on the front\nwall of the building.[10]\n\nThe interior of the Curia Julia is fairly austere. The hall is 25.2 m \u00d7 17.61\nm (82.7 ft \u00d7 57.8 ft). There are three broad steps that could have fitted five\nrows of chairs or a total of about 300 senators.[8] The walls are stripped but\nwere originally veneered in marble two thirds of the way up. The two main\nfeatures of the interior of the Curia Julia are its Altar of Victory and its\nstriking floor.\n\nAt the far end of the hall could be found the \"Altar of\n[Victory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_\\(mythology\\) \"Victoria\n\\(mythology\\)\")\".[8] It consisted of a statue of Victoria, the personification\nof victory, standing on a [globe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globe\n\"Globe\"), extending a [wreath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wreath \"Wreath\").\nThe altar was placed in the Curia by Augustus to celebrate Rome's military\nprowess, more specifically his own victory at the [Battle of\nActium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Actium \"Battle of Actium\"), in\n31 BC. The altar was removed in 384 AD, as part of a general backlash against\nthe pagan traditions of [Ancient\nRome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome \"Ancient Rome\") after the\nrise of [Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity\n\"Christianity\").[11]\n\nThe other main feature of the Curia's interior, the floor, is in contrast to\nthe building's colorless exterior. Featured on the floor is the Roman art\ntechnique of [opus sectile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opus_sectile \"Opus\nsectile\") in which materials are cut and inlaid into walls and floors to make\npictures of patterns.
