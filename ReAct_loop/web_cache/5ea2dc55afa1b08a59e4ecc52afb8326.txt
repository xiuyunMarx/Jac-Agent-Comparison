Fiction\")  \nWebsite| [jayneannephillips.com](http://jayneannephillips.com/)  \n  \n**Jayne Anne Phillips** (born July 19, 1952)[1] is an American novelist and\nshort story writer who was born in the small town of [Buckhannon, West\nVirginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buckhannon,_West_Virginia \"Buckhannon,\nWest Virginia\"). She was a professor of English at [Rutgers-\nNewark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutgers-Newark \"Rutgers-Newark\") from\n2005 to 2020 and helped establish the MFA program at [Rutgers University-\nNewark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutgers_University-Newark \"Rutgers\nUniversity-Newark\").\n\n## Education\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Jayne_Anne_Phillips&action=edit&section=1 \"Edit\nsection: Education\")]\n\nPhillips graduated from [West Virginia\nUniversity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Virginia_University \"West\nVirginia University\"), earning a [B.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B.A.\n\"B.A.\") in 1974, and later received an\n[M.F.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Fine_Arts \"Master of Fine\nArts\") in fiction from the [Iowa Writers'\nWorkshop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_Writers'_Workshop \"Iowa Writers'\nWorkshop\") at the [University of\nIowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Iowa \"University of Iowa\").\n\n## Teaching\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Jayne_Anne_Phillips&action=edit&section=2 \"Edit\nsection: Teaching\")]\n\nPhillips has held teaching positions at several colleges and universities,\nincluding [Harvard\nUniversity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University \"Harvard\nUniversity\"), [Williams\nCollege](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Williams_College \"Williams College\"),\nBrandeis University, and [Boston\nUniversity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_University \"Boston\nUniversity\"). She is currently Professor of English and founder/director of\nthe [Rutgers\nUniversity\u2013Newark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutgers_University\u2013Newark\n\"Rutgers University\u2013Newark\") Master of Fine Arts in Creative Writing\nProgram.[2] In 2007, _[The\nAtlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Atlantic \"The Atlantic\")_ magazine\nnamed Phillips' MFA program at Rutgers\u2013Newark in its list of \"Five Up-and-\nComing\" creative writing programs in the United States.[3]\n\n## Writing career\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Jayne_Anne_Phillips&action=edit&section=3 \"Edit\nsection: Writing career\")]\n\n### Early career\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Jayne_Anne_Phillips&action=edit&section=4 \"Edit\nsection: Early career\")]\n\nIn the mid-1970s, Phillips left West Virginia for California, embarking on a\ncross-country trip in the course of which she had numerous jobs, experiences,\nand encounters that would greatly influence her fiction, with its focus on\nlonely, lost souls and struggling survivors.\n\nIn 1976, Truck Press published her first short story collection _Sweethearts_\n, for which Phillips earned a [Pushcart\nPrize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pushcart_Prize \"Pushcart Prize\") and the\nCoordinating Council of Literary Magazines Fels Award.\n\n_Sweethearts_ was followed in 1978 by a second small-press collection,\n_Counting_ , issued by Vehicle Editions. _Counting_ earned Phillips greater\nrecognition and the St. Lawrence Award.\n\nHer next collection, _[Black\nTickets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Tickets \"Black Tickets\")_ ,\npublished by Delacorte/[Seymour\nLawrence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delacorte_Press \"Delacorte Press\") in\n1979 when she was 26, was her first book of stories and brought her national\nattention as a talented and important writer. _Black Tickets_ contained three\ntypes of stories: one-page fictions, inner soliloquies, and family dramas.\nThese stories focused on the characters' loneliness, alienation, and\nunsuccessful search for happiness. _Black Tickets_ is mentioned in the 2006\nlectures for the Modern Scholar series installment _From Here to Infinity_ ,\nby Professor [Michael D. C.\nDrout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_D._C._Drout \"Michael D. C.\nDrout\"), who refers to her style \u2014\u2014 he asserts it had a direct influence on\n[William Gibson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Gibson \"William\nGibson\")'s 1984 [cyberpunk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyberpunk\n\"Cyberpunk\") novel _[Neuromancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuromancer\n\"Neuromancer\")_ \u2014 as a \"headlong rush of story and description\".[4] Called\n\"the unmistakable work of early genius\" by Tillie Olsen, _Black Tickets_ was\npraised by [Raymond Carver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raymond_Carver\n\"Raymond Carver\"): \"These stories of America's disenfranchised \u2013 men and women\nlight-years away from the American Dream \u2013 are quite unlike any in our\nliterature ... this book is a crooked beauty.\" _Black Tickets_ was awarded the\nSue Kaufman Prize for First Fiction by the [American Academy and Institute of\nArts and\nLetters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_and_Institute_of_Arts_and_Letters\n\"American Academy and Institute of Arts and Letters\").[_[citation\nneeded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed\n\"Wikipedia:Citation needed\")_]\n\nIn 1984, Phillips published her first novel, _Machine Dreams_ , \"a remarkable\nnovelistic debut and an enduring literary achievement,\" according to the [_New\nYork\nTimes_.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Times.?action=edit&redlink=1\n\"New York Times. \\(page does not exist\\)\") _Machine Dreams_ is a chronicle of\nthe Hampson family from World War II to the Vietnam War. A [National Book\nCritics Circle\nAward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Book_Critics_Circle_Award\n\"National Book Critics Circle Award\") Finalist in Fiction, it was named one of\n12 Best Books of the Year cited by the _New York Times_. It appeared on\nseveral Bestseller lists and was optioned as a film by actor [Jessica\nLange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Lange \"Jessica Lange\"), who wrote\nthe screenplay. Nobel Prize winner [Nadine\nGordimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nadine_Gordimer \"Nadine Gordimer\")\nsaid that _Machine Dreams_ \"reaches one's deepest emotions. No number of books\nread or films seen can deaden one to the intimate act of art by which this\nwonderful young writer has penetrated the definitive experience of her\ngeneration.\" _[Fast\nLanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fast_Lanes?action=edit&redlink=1 \"Fast\nLanes \\(page does not exist\\)\")_ , a 1987 collection of ten stories, all\nfirst-person narratives, was praised as work by a writer \"in love with the\nAmerican language.\" One of the stories from _Fast Lanes_ , \"Rayme,\" had been\npublished in 1984 in _Granta'_ s [Dirty\nRealism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirty_realism \"Dirty realism\") issue.\n\nIn 1994, Phillips published her second novel, _Shelter,_ a portrait of the\nloss of innocence at a West Virginia girls' camp in the summer of 1963. Called\n\"a rich, vivid novel of moral and psychological complexity destined to stand\nalongside works by Faulkner and other Southern masters\" (_Vanity Fair_) and \"a\ndefiant, frighteningly beautiful novel as disturbing as its setting, _Shelter_\nfeels like Phillips' bid for immortality\" (_Harper's Bazaar_). Shelter was\nawarded an Academy Award in Literature by the American Academy and Institute\nof Arts and Letters.[_[citation\nneeded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed\n\"Wikipedia:Citation needed\")_]\n\n### Later career\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Jayne_Anne_Phillips&action=edit&section=5 \"Edit\nsection: Later career\")]\n\nPhillips' next novel was\n_[MotherKind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MotherKind?action=edit&redlink=1\n\"MotherKind \\(page does not exist\\)\")_ (2000), winner of the [Massachusetts\nBook Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Book_Award\n\"Massachusetts Book Award\"), a story of intergenerational love and struggles\nwithin a family facing many changes. It is praised as one of the best novels\nabout mothers and infants and the mother/daughter bond.\n\n_[Lark and\nTermite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lark_and_Termite?action=edit&redlink=1\n\"Lark and Termite \\(page does not exist\\)\")_ , her fourth novel, was published\nby [Knopf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_A._Knopf \"Alfred A. Knopf\") in\n2009 to positive reviews and was selected as one of five finalists for the\n[National Book Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Book_Award\n\"National Book Award\") in fiction.[5] _Lark and Termite_ was also a Finalist\nfor the National Book Critics Circle in Fiction; _Lark et Termite_ (French\ntranslation by Marc Amfreville) was a Finalist for the Prix de Medici\nEtrangers (Paris).\n\n_Quiet Dell_ , Phillips' fifth novel, based on the true story of the 1931\nmurders of Chicago widow Asta Eicher and her three children in the hamlet of\nQuiet Dell, West Virginia, is a fictional portrayal of one of the nation's\nfirst sensationalized serial murders. _Quiet Dell_ takes as its protagonist\nnine-year-old Annabel Eicher (victim, with her family, of con man [Harry\nPowers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Powers \"Harry Powers\"), who found\nhis victims through Depression-era matrimonial agencies) and Emily Thornhill,\na _[Chicago Tribune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Tribune \"Chicago\nTribune\")_ journalist who commits herself to finding justice for the Eichers.\nA Kirkus Review Fiction Pick of the Year and _Wall Street Journal_ Best Book\nof the Year, _Quiet Dell_ was called \"a story both splendid and irreparably\nsad\" by the _Chicago Tribune_ : \"As Phillips has proved throughout her decades\nof fiction writing, there is evil in the world but there are some who will\nstand in its way.\" _Quiet Dell_ was praised by the _Philadelphia Review of\nBooks_ : \"It is the texture of the telling that elevates this recounting from\ntrue crime to the realm of literary eminence.\"\n\nPhillips' works have been translated and published in twelve foreign\nlanguages.\n\n## Awards and honours\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Jayne_Anne_Phillips&action=edit&section=6 \"Edit\nsection: Awards and honours\")]\n\nShe is the recipient of a [Guggenheim\nFellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Fellowship \"Guggenheim\nFellowship\"), two [National Endowment for the\nArts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Endowment_for_the_Arts \"National\nEndowment for the Arts\") Fellowships, a Bunting Fellowship from the Bunting\nInstitute of [Radcliffe\nCollege](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radcliffe_College \"Radcliffe College\"),\na Rockefeller Foundation Bellagio Fellowship, and numerous other awards. In\n2024, she was awarded the [Pulitzer Prize for\nFiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulitzer_Prize_for_Fiction \"Pulitzer\nPrize for Fiction\") for her novel _[Night\nWatch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Night_Watch_\\(Phillips_novel\\) \"Night\nWatch \\(Phillips novel\\)\")_.[6]\n\n## Selected works\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Jayne_Anne_Phillips&action=edit&section=7 \"Edit\nsection: Selected works\")]\n\n### Short fiction\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Jayne_Anne_Phillips&action=edit&section=8 \"Edit\nsection: Short fiction\")]\n\n  * _Sweethearts_ (1976), prose vignettes\n  * _Counting_ (1978), prose vignettes\n  * _Black Tickets_ (1979), short story collection\n  * _How Mickey Made It_ (1981), short story\n  * _The Secret Country_ (1982), short story\n  * _Fast Lanes_ (1987), short story collection\n\n### Novels\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Jayne_Anne_Phillips&action=edit&section=9 \"Edit\nsection: Novels\")]\n\n  * _Machine Dreams_ (1984)[1]\n  * _Shelter_ (1994)[1]\n  * _MotherKind_ (2000)[1]\n  * _Lark and Termite_ (2009)[7]\n  * _Quiet Dell_(2013)[8]\n  * _[Night Watch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Night_Watch_\\(Phillips_novel\\) \"Night Watch \\(Phillips novel\\)\")_ (2023)[9]\n\n## References\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Jayne_Anne_Phillips&action=edit&section=10 \"Edit\nsection: References\")]\n\n  1. 1 2 3 4 [\"Phillips, Jayne Anne 1952\u2013 | Encyclopedia.com\"](http://www.encyclopedia.com/article-1G2-2590000539/phillips-jayne-anne-1952.html).\n  2. \u2191 [\"Outstanding Faculty | Rutgers University - Newark\"](https://web.archive.org/web/20100619190132/http://www.newark.rutgers.edu/ourfaculty/index.php?sId=kudosDetail&expertId=201).
