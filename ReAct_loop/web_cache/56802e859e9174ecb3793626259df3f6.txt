It is the second largest car manufacturer in India by sales until\n2025.[4]\n\n## History\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Hyundai_Motor_India&action=edit&section=1 \"Edit\nsection: History\")]\n\n[![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/02/HyundaiChennai.JPG/250px-\nHyundaiChennai.JPG?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:HyundaiChennai.JPG)Hyundai's\nmanufacturing plant near [Chennai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chennai\n\"Chennai\").\n[![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/23/Hyundai_Cars_at_the_port.jpg/250px-\nHyundai_Cars_at_the_port.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Hyundai_Cars_at_the_port.jpg)Hyundai\nCars at a port.\n\nIn the early 1990s, the [Indian automobile\nmarket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automotive_industry_in_India \"Automotive\nindustry in India\") had only five major automobile manufacturers: [Maruti\nSuzuki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maruti_Suzuki \"Maruti Suzuki\"),\n[Tata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TATA_Motors \"TATA Motors\") and\n[Mahindra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahindra_&_Mahindra_Limited \"Mahindra\n& Mahindra Limited\"),\n[Hindustan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindustan_Motors \"Hindustan\nMotors\"), [Premier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premier_Automobiles_Limited\n\"Premier Automobiles Limited\"). Maruti Suzuki had a near\n[monopoly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monopoly \"Monopoly\") over the\npassenger cars segment. Tata and Mahindra were solely utility and commercial\nvehicle manufacturers, while Hindustan and Premier both built outdated and\nuncompetitive products.[_[citation\nneeded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed\n\"Wikipedia:Citation needed\")_]\n[Daewoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daewoo_Motors \"Daewoo Motors\") had\nentered the Indian automobile market with\n[Cielo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daewoo_Cielo \"Daewoo Cielo\") just three\nyears back, while\n[Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_India_Private_Limited \"Ford India\nPrivate Limited\"), [Opel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opel \"Opel\") and\n[Honda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_Siel_Cars_India \"Honda Siel Cars\nIndia\") had entered less than a year back.[_[citation\nneeded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed\n\"Wikipedia:Citation needed\")_]\n\nHyundai Motor India Limited was formed on 6 May 1996 as a wholly owned\nsubsidiary of [Hyundai Motor\nCompany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyundai_Motor_Company \"Hyundai Motor\nCompany\") of [South Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Korea \"South\nKorea\"). Its first manufacturing plant was constructed in\n[Irungattukottai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irungattukottai\n\"Irungattukottai\") near [Chennai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chennai\n\"Chennai\"), [Tamil Nadu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil_Nadu \"Tamil\nNadu\").[5] Its first car, the [Hyundai\nSantro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyundai_Santro \"Hyundai Santro\"), was\nlaunched on 23 September 1998 and was a runaway success. Within a few months\nof its inception, HMIL became India's second-largest automobile manufacturer\nand the largest automobile exporter.\n\nMr Yang Soo Kim was then the managing director of Hyundai Motor\nIndia.[_[citation\nneeded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed\n\"Wikipedia:Citation needed\")_] The Indian counterpart leadership team was\nmanaged by Mr AP Gandhi, Mr BVR Subbu and Mr G.S. Ramesh. Mr AP Gandhi was the\npresident for a few years of inception. Mr BVR Subbu was the marketing\ndirector responsible for dealer development and sales. He resigned from\nHyundai Motor India in 2006.[6]\n\nHMC has set up a research and development facility (Hyundai Motor India\nEngineering \u2013 HMIE) in the cyber city of Hyderabad.\n\nAs HMC's global export hub for compact cars, HMIL became the first automotive\ncompany in India to achieve the export of 10 lakh cars in just over a decade.\nHMIL currently exports cars to more than 87 countries.[7]\n\nIn July 2012, [Arvind Saxena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arvind_Saxena\n\"Arvind Saxena\"), the Director of Marketing and Sales stepped down from the\nposition after serving the company for seven years.[8]\n\nIn August 2018, Rakesh Srivastava, Director of Sales and Marketing at Hyundai\nIndia, resigned from his position after serving for six years.\n\nOn 4 December 2018, Hyundai Motor India Ltd, announced the top-level\nmanagement changes immediately. Seon Seob Kim assumed the responsibilities of\nMD and CEO from Young Key Koo.[_[citation\nneeded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed\n\"Wikipedia:Citation needed\")_]\n\nIn 2019, Hyundai Motor Company invested around 1.1 trillion KRW in an\nadditional EV production line and began manufacturing Kona Electric.\n\nIn 2022, Hyundai Motor India Limited receives about 85% of its electrical\npower as eco-friendly energy from external power plants. Moreover, it is\ncurrently installing a stationary solar power system of 1.9MWp using the\nmodule factory's roof with a size of 13,028m\u00b2.\n\nOn 15 June 2024, Hyundai Motor India filed a draft prospectus with the\n[Securities and Exchange Board of\nIndia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Securities_and_Exchange_Board_of_India\n\"Securities and Exchange Board of India\") seeking approval for an initial\npublic offering.[9]\n\nAS of 2025, HMIL is the second largest passenger car exporter and the second\nlargest car manufacturer in India.[10]\n\n## Manufacturing facilities\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Hyundai_Motor_India&action=edit&section=2 \"Edit\nsection: Manufacturing facilities\")]\n\nHMIL has two manufacturing plants, one in\n[Irungattukottai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irungattukottai\n\"Irungattukottai\"), [Tamil Nadu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil_Nadu\n\"Tamil Nadu\") and Other in [Talegaon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talegaon\n\"Talegaon\"), [Maharashtra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maharashtra\n\"Maharashtra\").\n[Irungattukottai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irungattukottai\n\"Irungattukottai\") Plant has a capacity of 850,000 vehicles per annum.[11]\n\n## R&D centre\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Hyundai_Motor_India&action=edit&section=3 \"Edit\nsection: R&D centre\")]\n\nHyundai Motor India Engineering (HMIE) is a fully owned subsidiary of Hyundai\nMotor Company, South Korea, which is located at Hyderabad, Telangana. HMIL\nestablished HMIE in November 2006 and contributed to the development of\nHyundai Motors' popular new models for the Indian market starting with the Eon\nand followed now by the \"i\" series, and also in SUV segments like the Creta.\nHyundai Motors' other overseas R&D centres are located in the United States,\nGermany, Japan, Korea, and China.[_[citation\nneeded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed\n\"Wikipedia:Citation needed\")_]\n\n## Global Quality Centre\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Hyundai_Motor_India&action=edit&section=4 \"Edit\nsection: Global Quality Centre\")]\n\n[![icon](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/9/99/Question_book-\nnew.svg/60px-Question_book-\nnew.svg.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Question_book-\nnew.svg)| This section **does\nnot[cite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citing_sources\n\"Wikipedia:Citing sources\") any\n[sources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Verifiability\n\"Wikipedia:Verifiability\")**.
