height ranking| 123  \n  \n**Kaieteur Falls** is one of the most powerful single-drop\n[waterfalls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterfall \"Waterfall\")[1] in the\nworld. It is located on the [Potaro\nRiver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potaro_River \"Potaro River\") in [Kaieteur\nNational Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaieteur_National_Park \"Kaieteur\nNational Park\"), central Essequibo Territory,\n[Guyana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guyana \"Guyana\"). It is 226 metres (741\nft) high when measured from its plunge over a\n[sandstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandstone \"Sandstone\") and\n[conglomerate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conglomerate_\\(geology\\)\n\"Conglomerate \\(geology\\)\") cliff to the first break. It then flows over a\nseries of steep cascades that, when included in the measurements, bring the\ntotal height to 251 metres (822 ft). While many falls have greater height or\ngreater flow, few have the combination of height and water volume, and\nKaieteur is among the most powerful waterfalls in the world with an average\nflow rate of 663 cubic meters per second (23,400 cubic feet per second).[2]\nKaieteur Falls is about four and a half times the height of [Niagara\nFalls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niagara_Falls \"Niagara Falls\"), on the\nborder between [Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada \"Canada\") and the\n[United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States \"United States\"),\nand about twice the height of [Victoria\nFalls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_Falls \"Victoria Falls\"), on the\nborder of [Zambia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zambia \"Zambia\") and\n[Zimbabwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zimbabwe \"Zimbabwe\") in\n[Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Africa \"Africa\").\n\nUpriver from the falls, the [Potaro\nPlateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potaro_Plateau?action=edit&redlink=1\n\"Potaro Plateau \\(page does not exist\\)\") stretches out to the distant\n[escarpment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escarpment \"Escarpment\") of the\n[Pakaraima Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakaraima_Mountains\n\"Pakaraima Mountains\"). The Potaro River empties into the [Essequibo\nRiver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essequibo_River \"Essequibo River\") which\nis the 34th longest river in South America and the longest river in Guyana.\n\n## History and discovery\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Kaieteur_Falls&action=edit&section=1 \"Edit section:\nHistory and discovery\")]\n\nLong known about by Indigenous nations in the area, the falls were noticed by\nEuropeans in 1870 by a party led by [Charles Barrington\nBrown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Barrington_Brown \"Charles\nBarrington Brown\"), a British geologist appointed as a government surveyor to\nthe colony of [British Guiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Guiana\n\"British Guiana\"). Brown and his partner [James\nSawkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Gay_Sawkins \"James Gay Sawkins\")\nhad arrived in [Georgetown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgetown,_Guyana\n\"Georgetown, Guyana\") in 1867; they worked both together and independently,\nwhich resulted in Brown discovering the falls alone. He did not have time to\ninvestigate Kaieteur Falls closely, so he returned one year later to make\ncomprehensive measurements.[3] Brown's book _Canoe and Camp life in British\nGuiana_ was published in 1876. Two years later, in 1878, he published _Fifteen\nThousand Miles on the Amazon and its tributaries_.\n\nAccording to a [Patamona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patamona \"Patamona\")\nIndian legend, Kaieteur Falls was named for Kuik'tuwuk old man or man, a\n[Toshao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toshao?action=edit&redlink=1 \"Toshao\n\\(page does not exist\\)\") (chief) who sacrificed himself.\n[Makonaima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makonaima?action=edit&redlink=1\n\"Makonaima \\(page does not exist\\)\") is a god for Patomana people so by\npaddling over the falls to save his people. Another legend though was told to\nBrown by indigenous people on the night of discovery of the falls. Thus, the\nfall was named \"Kaieteur\"(kuik-man, tu'wuk -large place, falls means e'me'l\u00fa\nwhich means \"old-man-fall\").\n\n## Tourism\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Kaieteur_Falls&action=edit&section=2 \"Edit section:\nTourism\")]\n\nKaieteur Falls is a major tourist attraction in Guyana. It is in [Kaieteur\nNational Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaieteur_National_Park \"Kaieteur\nNational Park\") in the centre of Guyana's rainforest. The park is served by\n[Kaieteur International\nAirport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaieteur_International_Airport\n\"Kaieteur International Airport\"), about a 15-minute walk from the top of\nKaieteur falls, with frequent flights to [Ogle\nAirport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ogle_Airport \"Ogle Airport\") and\n[Cheddi Jagan International\nAirport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheddi_Jagan_International_Airport\n\"Cheddi Jagan International Airport\") in Georgetown.\n\n## Popular culture\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Kaieteur_Falls&action=edit&section=3 \"Edit section:\nPopular culture\")]\n\nKaieteur Falls is featured in:\n\n  * the documentary film _[The White Diamond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_White_Diamond \"The White Diamond\")_ by [Werner Herzog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Werner_Herzog \"Werner Herzog\") and Animal Planet's _[River Monsters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Monsters \"River Monsters\")_ with [Jeremy Wade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeremy_Wade \"Jeremy Wade\").\n  * the science fiction animation television series _[G Gundam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G_Gundam \"G Gundam\")_. The lead character trains and bathes under the falls before his final fight.\n  * the opening of the fourth season credits of the Discovery Channel's series _Gold Rush_ (2013).
