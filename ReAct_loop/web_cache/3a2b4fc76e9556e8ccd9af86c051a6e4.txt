this on Wikidata\")  \n  \n**Person of the Year** , called **Man of the Year** or **Woman of the Year**\nuntil 1999,[1] is an annual issue of the American [news\nmagazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_magazine \"News magazine\") and\nwebsite _[Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_\\(magazine\\) \"Time\n\\(magazine\\)\")_ featuring a person, group, idea, or object that \"for better or\nfor worse ... has done the most to influence the events of the year\".[2] The\n_Time_ website or a partner organization also runs an annual online reader's\npoll that has no effect on the selection, although no poll was held in 2023 or\n2024.[3][4]\n\n## Background\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Time_Person_of_the_Year&action=edit&section=1 \"Edit\nsection: Background\")]\n\nThe tradition of selecting a \"Man of the Year\" began privately in 1927, with\n_Time_ editors contemplating the news makers of the year after a series of\n\"slow news days\" leading up to New Year's Day.[4] The idea originally focused\non a Man of the Week before it was decided to use [Charles\nLindbergh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Lindbergh \"Charles\nLindbergh\") to represent the predominant story of 1927, with the magazine\nlisting him as Man of the Year being published in early 1928.[4] The idea was\nalso an attempt to remedy the editorial embarrassment earlier that year of not\nhaving aviator Lindbergh on its cover following his historic [transatlantic\nflight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transatlantic_flight \"Transatlantic\nflight\").[4] By the end of the year, it was decided that a cover story\nfeaturing Lindbergh as the Man of the Year would serve both purposes.[5]\nBefore the online poll was instituted, \"readers were invited to weigh in by\nmail.\"[4]\n\n## Selection\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Time_Person_of_the_Year&action=edit&section=2 \"Edit\nsection: Selection\")]\n\n### State leaders\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Time_Person_of_the_Year&action=edit&section=3 \"Edit\nsection: State leaders\")]\n\nSince the list began, every serving president of the United States has been a\nMan or Person of the Year at least once, with the exceptions of [Calvin\nCoolidge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calvin_Coolidge \"Calvin Coolidge\")\n(whose last term began before the first issue), [Herbert\nHoover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_Hoover \"Herbert Hoover\") (the\nsubsequent president), and [Gerald\nFord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerald_Ford \"Gerald Ford\") (the only\npresident never to have been elected to the office of president or vice\npresident). Most were named Man or Person of the Year either the year they\nwere elected or while they were in office; the only one to be given the title\nbefore being elected was [Dwight D.\nEisenhower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dwight_D._Eisenhower \"Dwight D.\nEisenhower\"), in 1944, as [Supreme Commander of the Allied Invasion\nForce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Headquarters_Allied_Expeditionary_Force\n\"Supreme Headquarters Allied Expeditionary Force\"), eight years before his\nfirst election. He received the title again in 1959 while in office. [Franklin\nD. Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_D._Roosevelt \"Franklin D.\nRoosevelt\") became the first chosen US president and is the only person to\nhave received the title three times, first as [president-\nelect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President-elect_of_the_United_States\n\"President-elect of the United States\") (1932) and later as the incumbent\npresident (1934 and 1941).
