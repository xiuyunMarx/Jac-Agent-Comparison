Prison in Rome, with an altar commemorating the imprisonment of\n[Saints Peter and Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saints_Peter_and_Paul\n\"Saints Peter and Paul\") there\n\nThe **Mamertine Prison**\n([Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_language \"Italian language\"):\n_Carcere Mamertino_), in antiquity the **Tullianum** , was a prison (_carcer_)\nwith a dungeon (_[oubliette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dungeon\n\"Dungeon\")_) located in the [Comitium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comitium\n\"Comitium\") in [ancient Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome\n\"Ancient Rome\"). It is said to have been built in the 7th century BC and was\nsituated on the northeastern slope of the [Capitoline\nHill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitoline_Hill \"Capitoline Hill\"), facing\nthe [Curia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curia \"Curia\") and the imperial\n[forums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forum_\\(Roman\\) \"Forum \\(Roman\\)\") of\n[Nerva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nerva \"Nerva\"),\n[Vespasian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vespasian \"Vespasian\"), and\n[Augustus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustus \"Augustus\"). Located between\nit and the [Tabularium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabularium \"Tabularium\")\n(record house) were the [Gemonian\nstairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gemonian_stairs \"Gemonian stairs\")\nleading to the [Arx of the\nCapitoline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arx_\\(Roman\\) \"Arx \\(Roman\\)\").\n\nThe church of [San Giuseppe dei\nFalegnami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Giuseppe_dei_Falegnami \"San\nGiuseppe dei Falegnami\") now stands above the Mamertine.[1]\n\n## Name and origin\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Mamertine_Prison&action=edit&section=1 \"Edit\nsection: Name and origin\")]\n\nThe origins of the prison's names are uncertain. The traditional derivation of\n\"Tullianum\" is from the name of one of the Roman kings [Tullus\nHostilius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tullus_Hostilius \"Tullus Hostilius\")\nor [Servius Tullius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Servius_Tullius \"Servius\nTullius\") (the latter is found in [Livy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livy\n\"Livy\"), [Varro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcus_Terentius_Varro \"Marcus\nTerentius Varro\"), and also [Sallust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sallust\n\"Sallust\")); there is an alternative theory that it is from the [archaic\nLatin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Latin \"Old Latin\") _tullius_ \"a jet\nof water\", in reference to the cistern. The name \"Mamertine\" is medieval in\norigin, and may be a reference to a nearby temple of Mars.\n\nAccording to tradition, the prison was constructed around 640\u2013616 BC, by\n[Ancus Marcius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancus_Marcius \"Ancus Marcius\").\nIt was originally created as a [cistern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cistern\n\"Cistern\") for a spring in the floor of the second lower level. Prisoners were\nlowered through an opening into the lower\n[oubliette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dungeon \"Dungeon\"), known as the\nTullianum.\n\n## Use\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Mamertine_Prison&action=edit&section=2 \"Edit\nsection: Use\")]\n\n[Imprisonment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imprisonment \"Imprisonment\") was\nnot a sentence under [Roman statutory\nlaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_law \"Roman law\"),[2] though\n[detention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detention_\\(imprisonment\\)\n\"Detention \\(imprisonment\\)\") is mentioned in the [Twelve\nTables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve_Tables \"Twelve Tables\") and\nthroughout the [Digest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digest_\\(Roman_law\\)\n\"Digest \\(Roman law\\)\").[3] \"Detention\", however, includes [debt\nbondage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nexum \"Nexum\") in the early\nRepublic;[4] the wearing of chains (_vincula publica_), mainly for\n[slaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery_in_ancient_Rome \"Slavery in\nancient Rome\"); and during the [Imperial\nera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire \"Roman Empire\") a sentence of\n[hard labor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penal_labour \"Penal labour\") at the\nmills, mines or quarries.[5] Slaves or lower-status citizens sentenced to hard\nlabor were held in prison camps.[6]\n\n[![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/51/Illustrerad_Verldshistoria_band_II_Ill_022.png/250px-\nIllustrerad_Verldshistoria_band_II_Ill_022.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Illustrerad_Verldshistoria_band_II_Ill_022.png)\nMamertine Prison oubliette. From _Illustrated world history_ , Volume II, by\nErnst Wallis.\n\nIncarceration (_publica custodia_) in facilities such as the Tullianum was\nintended to be a temporary measure prior to trial or execution; abuses of this\nprinciple occurred but were officially censured.[7] Located near the law\ncourts, the Tullianum was used as a jail or holding cell for short periods\nbefore executions and as a site for executions. In 63 BC, certain [co-\nconspirators of Catiline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catiline_Conspiracy\n\"Catiline Conspiracy\"), including [Publius Cornelius Lentulus\nSura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Publius_Cornelius_Lentulus_\\(Sura\\)\n\"Publius Cornelius Lentulus \\(Sura\\)\"), were held briefly in the Tullianum and\nexecuted there for their alleged plot to overthrow the government.[8] In this\ncase, the executions were conducted hastily, without due process of appeal,\nduring the consulship of [Cicero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cicero\n\"Cicero\"), who was later exiled for his actions.[9]\n[Sejanus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sejanus \"Sejanus\") was held in the\nTullianum before his baroque execution, which involved the [Gemonian\nstairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gemonian_stairs \"Gemonian stairs\"), and\nthe [conflicting accounts of the end of\nPleminius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quintus_Pleminius#Questions_of_arrest_and_exile\n\"Quintus Pleminius\") include a timely death in jail during trial. Some\n[Gracchan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gracchi \"Gracchi\") sympathizers ended\nup in the _Carcer_ , where the\n[haruspex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haruspex \"Haruspex\") Herennius\nSiculus hit his head on an\n[architrave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architrave \"Architrave\") and died\nbefore he could be executed.[10]\n\nThere is no evidence that the Tullianum was used for long-term incarceration,\nand the lowest dungeon was unsuited for the purpose; the level above, however,\nin theory might have been.[11] In general, long-term incarceration was more\nwidely practiced in the later Empire, and from the 4th century, under\nChristian rule, Roman laws and occasional personal intervention on the part of\nan [emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_emperor \"Roman emperor\")\nindicate a growing need to crack down on abuses such as filthy conditions and\ntorture.[12]\n\nIn some cases, it is unclear whether a source using the word _carcer_ means\n\"the\" Carcer, or imprisonment in some other facility. High-status prisoners,\nwhether Roman or foreign, were typically held in the custody of individual\nRomans, sometimes at [their homes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domus\n\"Domus\") or [country estates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_villa \"Roman\nvilla\"). The line between being a war captive and a hostage lawfully held by\ntreaty was thin, and conditions of captivity could vary widely, from abject\nmisery and humiliation to relative luxury. As a prisoner of war, [Perseus of\nMacedon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perseus_of_Macedon \"Perseus of\nMacedon\") was placed in a foul, overcrowded dungeon at [Alba\nFucens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alba_Fucens \"Alba Fucens\");[13] the son\nof [Tigranes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigranes_the_Great \"Tigranes the\nGreat\") was kept at a [praetor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Praetor\n\"Praetor\")'s house in Rome, where he could be trotted out as a dinner-party\nguest.[14] The Tullianum only rarely played a role in these detentions.\nCaptured foreign rulers or generals were paraded in a Roman conqueror's\n[triumph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_triumph \"Roman triumph\"), and on\na few occasions the \"most prominent, famous, or dastardly\" were executed\nafterward at the Tullianum.[15] These were \"strikingly few\" in number, and\nincluded the [Samnite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samnium \"Samnium\") [Gaius\nPontius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaius_Pontius \"Gaius Pontius\"), the\n[Gaul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arverni \"Arverni\")\n[Vercingetorix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vercingetorix \"Vercingetorix\"),\nsome [\"Cilician\" pirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cilician_pirates\n\"Cilician pirates\"), and the [Galatian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galatia\n\"Galatia\") [Adiatorix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adiatorix\n\"Adiatorix\").[16] [Jugurtha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jugurtha\n\"Jugurtha\"), king of [Numidia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numidia\n\"Numidia\"), may have been executed at the conclusion of\n[Marius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaius_Marius \"Gaius Marius\")'s triumph,\nor he may have died in prison several days afterward.[17] Most high-status war\ncaptives were neither executed nor held for any substantial length of time in\nthe Tullianum.[18]\n\n## Christian significance\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Mamertine_Prison&action=edit&section=3 \"Edit\nsection: Christian significance\")]\n\n[![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/43/Entrancetoprison.jpg/250px-\nEntrancetoprison.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Entrancetoprison.jpg)The\nentrance to the prison records that Saint Peter and Saint Paul were imprisoned\nthere\n\nAlthough Saint Paul is said to have been held in Mamertine Prison, he awaited\ntrial in a house in the southern [Campus\nMartius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campus_Martius \"Campus Martius\") that\nbecame the church [San Paolo alla\nRegola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Paolo_alla_Regola \"San Paolo
