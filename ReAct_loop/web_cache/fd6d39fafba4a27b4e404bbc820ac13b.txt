talk:Pulitzer Prize sidebar\")\n  * [e](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special:EditPage/Template:Pulitzer_Prize_sidebar \"Special:EditPage/Template:Pulitzer Prize sidebar\")\n\n  \n  \nThe **Pulitzer Prize for Fiction** is one of the twenty-three American\n[Pulitzer Prizes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulitzer_Prize \"Pulitzer\nPrize\") that are annually awarded for Letters, Drama, and Music.[1] It\nrecognizes distinguished fiction by an American author, preferably dealing\nwith American life, published during the preceding calendar year.[2]\n\nAs the **Pulitzer Prize for the Novel** (awarded 1918\u20131947), it was one of the\noriginal Pulitzers; the program was\n[inaugurated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inauguration \"Inauguration\") in\n1917 with seven prizes, four of which were awarded that year[3] (no Novel\nprize was awarded in 1917, the first one having been granted in 1918).[4]\n\nThe name was changed to the **Pulitzer Prize for Fiction** in 1948[5] and\neligibility was expanded to the broader \"distinguished fiction published in\nbook form\".[6][7] The Advisory Board wanted to give the award to [James A.\nMichener](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_A._Michener \"James A.\nMichener\")'s _[Tales of the South\nPacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tales_of_the_South_Pacific \"Tales of\nthe South Pacific\")_ , which required expanding the category criteria and\nlifting the ban on short stories.[8]\n\nFinalists have been announced since 1980, usually a total of three.[4]\n\n## Definition\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Pulitzer_Prize_for_Fiction&action=edit&section=1\n\"Edit section: Definition\")]\n\nAs defined in the original Plan of Award, the prize was given \"Annually, for\nthe American novel published during the year which shall best present the\nwholesome atmosphere of American life, and the highest standard of American\nmanners and manhood,\" although there was some struggle over whether the word\n_wholesome_ should be used instead of _whole_ , the word Pulitzer had written\nin his will.[9] In 1927, the advisory board quietly instituted Pulitzer's word\nchoice, replacing _wholesome_ with _whole_.\n\nA new consideration arose when the Pulitzer jury was unanimous in recommending\n[Thornton Wilder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thornton_Wilder \"Thornton\nWilder\")'s _[The Bridge of San Luis\nRey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bridge_of_San_Luis_Rey \"The Bridge of\nSan Luis Rey\")_ for the 1928 prize, although the book deals with Peruvians in\n[Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peru \"Peru\"), not with Americans in\nAmerica. The jury chair, Richard Burton of [Columbia\nUniversity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University \"Columbia\nUniversity\"), emphasized the _moral_ value of the book in his report to the\nadvisory board: \"This piece of fiction is not only an admirable example of\nliterary skill in the art of fiction, but also possesses a philosophic import\nand a spiritual elevation which greatly increases its literary value.\" [Rober"}
