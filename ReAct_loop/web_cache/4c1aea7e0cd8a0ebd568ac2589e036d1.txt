Basilica of Santa Maria di Cosmedin and crossing the road,\nyou can enjoy the view of the Temple of Hercules Victor.\n\n  \nThe temple is protected by a large fence but it is still possible to see the\nbeauty of the whole structure.  \nThe construction of the temple dates back to the second century BC and today\nrepresents the oldest building in Rome still standing.\n\n![](https://www.italyrometour.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/temple-of-\nhercules-rome.jpeg)Temple of Hercules Rome\n\nThis temple is sometimes still referred to as the Temple of Vesta and the\nerror is due to its circular shape which makes it similar to the real Temple\nof Vesta located in the Roman Forum. All this due to an incorrect attribution,\nborn during the Renaissance.\n\nA curious peculiarity that makes it even more mysterious and fascinating.\n\nThe temple is circular in shape with twenty columns surrounding the\ncylindrical cell and rests on a ring foundation on a tuff block platform. A\nfavissa opens in the floor of the cell, a place where the ancient Romans\ndeposited votive objects, consisting of a deep well, where the burnt remains\nof sacrifices were collected.\n\n[![tripadvisor](https://www.italyrometour.com/wp-\ncontent/uploads/2024/05/tripadvisor.png)](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Attraction_Review-g187791-d2016486-Reviews-\nItaly_Rome_Tour-Rome_Lazio.html)What people said about Us\n\n  \nThis temple confirms the economic power that the Roman merchants had. In fact,\nit is said that a rich merchant commissioned this work which was dedicated to\nHercules protector of the oil producers, HERCULES OLIVARIUS, of the\ncorporation to which the merchant belonged.  \nOver time the temple was then converted into a church which was dedicated to\nSanto Stefano and subsequently changed its name to that of Santa Maria del\nSole.\n\nRoman Forum 3D Reconstruction\n\nThe Temple of Hercules Victor or Hercules Invitto or Ercole Oleario, is\nlocated in Piazza della Bocca della Verita in Rome, not far from the Temple of\nPortunus within the forum Boarium. Dating back to 120 BC approximately, it is\nthe oldest preserved marble building in Rome (the oldest of all was the temple\nof Jupiter Stator in the portico of Metello, from 146 BC, now lost).\n\nThe structure of the temple imitated that of the lost Temple of Hercules\nInvitto, built by Scipio Emiliano in 142 BC. near the Altar Massima and\ndestroyed in the 15th century (some drawings and plans by Baldassarre Peruzzi\nand others remain).\n\nThe whole structure betrays a clear derivation from Greek models (with the\nsteps instead of the podium and the marble structure), both in the\narchitecture and in the decorations, inspired by models of the 4th century BC,\nsuch as the tholoi of the great Greek sanctuaries, with the however, a filter\nof late Hellenism. It is a typical work of Neo-Attic artists who in the 2nd\ncentury BC. they operated in Rome (in works such as the Altar of Domizio\nEnobarbus or the Capitoline Acrolith of Juno Regina).\n\nThe temple is monoptero, circular in shape, and is built in marble. Its plant\nhas a diameter of 14.8 meters. The original marble used for the work is Greek,\nPentelic.\n\nIt stands on a ring foundation of cappellaccio blocks in turn on a platform\nmade of tuff blocks of Grotta Oscura, which incorporate the outlet of the\nCloaca Maxima.
