Nonfiction\n(1962\u2013present)\nMemoir or Autobiography\n(2023\u2013present)\nSpecial Awards\n(1918\u2013present)\nv\nt\ne\nThe\nPulitzer Prize for Fiction\nis one of the twenty-three American\nPulitzer Prizes\nthat are annually awarded for Letters, Drama, and Music.\n[\n1\n]\nIt recognizes distinguished fiction by an American author, preferably dealing with American life, published during the preceding calendar year.\n[\n2\n]\nAs the\nPulitzer Prize for the Novel\n(awarded 1918\u20131947), it was one of the original Pulitzers; the program was\ninaugurated\nin 1917 with seven prizes, four of which were awarded that year\n[\n3\n]\n(no Novel prize was awarded in 1917, the first one having been granted in 1918).\n[\n4\n]\nThe name was changed to the\nPulitzer Prize for Fiction\nin 1948\n[\n5\n]\nand eligibility was expanded to the broader \"distinguished fiction published in book form\".\n[\n6\n]\n[\n7\n]\nThe Advisory Board wanted to give the award to\nJames A. Michener\n's\nTales of the South Pacific\n, which required expanding the category criteria and lifting the ban on short stories.\n[\n8\n]\nFinalists have been announced since 1980, usually a total of three.\n[\n4\n]\nDefinition\n[\nedit\n]\nAs defined in the original Plan of Award, the prize was given \"Annually, for the American novel published during the year which shall best present the wholesome atmosphere of American life, and the highest standard of American manners and manhood,\" although there was some struggle over whether the word\nwholesome\nshould be used instead of\nwhole\n, the word Pulitzer had written in his will.\n[\n9\n]\nIn 1927, the advisory board quietly instituted Pulitzer's word choice, replacing\nwholesome\nwith\nwhole\n.\nA new consideration arose when the Pulitzer jury was unanimous in recommending\nThornton Wilder\n's\nThe Bridge of San Luis Rey\nfor the 1928 prize, although the book deals with Peruvians in\nPeru\n, not with Americans in America. The jury chair, Richard Burton of\nColumbia University\n, emphasized the\nmoral\nvalue of the book in his report to the advisory board: \"This piece of fiction is not only an admirable example of literary skill in the art of fiction, but also possesses a philosophic import and a spiritual elevation which greatly increases its literary value.\"\nRobert Morss Lovett\ndisagreed, saying it would be \"mere subterfuge to say that it has anything to do with the highest standard of American manners and manhood,\" but went along with the jury in finding \"less literary merit\" in the other novels under discussion. (Lovett rejected the runner-up\nBlack April\nby\nJulia Peterkin\n, calling it \"a rather unedifying picture of life in a primitive negro community\" and \"an ironical answer to the terms on which the prize is offered.\" Peterkin won nevertheless in 1929 for a similar novel,\nScarlet Sister Mary\n.) Having settled on\nBridge\n, the Advisory Board redefined the conditions from \"whole atmosphere of American life, and the highest standard of American manners and manhood\" to \"preferably one which shall best present the whole atmosphere of American life,\" although this did not address the novel's setting.\n[\n10\n]\nFurther refinement into \"the best novel published that year by an American author\" removed any impediment to\nPearl S. Buck\n's\nThe Good Earth\nin 1932, also with a foreign setting in its study of Chinese village life in\nAnhui\n,\nEast China\n.\n[\n11\n]\nWith 1929 came the first of several much more substantive changes. The board changed the wording to \"preferably one which shall best present the whole atmosphere of American life\" and deleted the insistence that the novel portray \"the highest standard of American manners and manhood\". In 1936, emphasis was changed again, with the award going to \"a distinguished novel published during the year by an American author, preferably dealing with American life\". In 1948, the advisory board widened the scope of the award with the wording \"For distinguished fiction published in book form during the year by an American author, preferably dealing with American life.\"\n[\n9\n]\nThis change allowed the prize to go to a collection of short stories for the first time, James Michener's\nTales of the South Pacific\n.\nWinners\n[\nedit\n]\nThis section\nneeds\nmore citations\n.\nPlease help\nimprove this section\nby\nadding citations to reliable sources\n.
