Wikidata \\[g\\]\")\n\nAppearance\n\nmove to sidebar hide\n\nFrom Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia\n\nOverview of the architecture in Rome\n\nThe **architecture of Rome** over the centuries has greatly developed from\n[Ancient Roman\narchitecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Roman_architecture\n\"Ancient Roman architecture\") to [Italian modern and contemporary\narchitecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_modern_and_contemporary_architecture\n\"Italian modern and contemporary architecture\").\n[Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome \"Rome\") was once the world's main\nepicentres of [Classical\narchitecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_architecture \"Classical\narchitecture\"), developing new forms such as the\n[arch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arch \"Arch\"), the\n[dome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dome \"Dome\") and the\n[vault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vault_\\(architecture\\) \"Vault\n\\(architecture\\)\"). The\n[Romanesque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanesque_architecture \"Romanesque\narchitecture\") style in the 11th, 12th and 13th centuries was also widely used\nin Roman [architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architecture\n\"Architecture\"), and later the city became one of the main centres of\n[Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance_architecture\n\"Renaissance architecture\") and [Baroque\narchitecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque_architecture \"Baroque\narchitecture\").[1] Rome's cityscape is also widely\n[Neoclassical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoclassical_architecture\n\"Neoclassical architecture\") and\n[Fascist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascist_architecture \"Fascist\narchitecture\") in style.\n\n## Ancient Rome\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Architecture_of_Rome&action=edit&section=1 \"Edit\nsection: Ancient Rome\")]\n\nMain article: [Ancient Roman\narchitecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Roman_architecture\n\"Ancient Roman architecture\")\n\n[![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/de/Colosseo_2020.jpg/250px-\nColosseo_2020.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Colosseo_2020.jpg)The\n[Colosseum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colosseum \"Colosseum\")\n\nDuring the [Roman Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Republic\n\"Roman Republic\"), most Roman buildings were made of concrete and bricks, but\never since about 100 BC and the [Roman\nEmpire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire \"Roman Empire\"), marble and\ngold were more widely used as decoration themes in the architecture of Rome,\nespecially in temples, palaces, fora and public buildings in general.[1] Most\nbuildings, just like in [Classical\nAthens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_Athens \"Classical Athens\"),\nhad [caryatids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caryatid \"Caryatid\") and\n[atlantes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlas_\\(architecture\\) \"Atlas\n\\(architecture\\)\") supporting balconies or structures, mainly representing\nstanding women or muscular men. The caryatids found in [ancient Roman\narchitecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Roman_architecture\n\"Ancient Roman architecture\") were mainly copies of the Greek ones, just like\nin the Forum of Augustus.[1]\n\n[Roman temples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_temple \"Roman temple\")\nwere usually built on a high podium. They had a\n[portico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portico \"Portico\") and a triangular\n[pediment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pediment \"Pediment\") above\ncolumns.[1] Romans mainly utilized the [Greek classical\norders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_order#Greek_orders \"Classical\norder\"), but also created a new [Composite\norder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Composite_order \"Composite order\"), which\nwas used in the [Arch of Titus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arch_of_Titus\n\"Arch of Titus\"). Domes, vaults,\n[aediculae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aediculae \"Aediculae\") (small\nshrines designed as small temples) and\n[coffers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffer \"Coffer\") were also popular\nelements of Classical and Imperial Roman architecture. The\n[Colosseum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colosseum \"Colosseum\") is the most\nprominent example of ancient Roman architecture, but also the [Roman\nForum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Forum \"Roman Forum\"), the [Domus\nAurea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domus_Aurea \"Domus Aurea\"), the\n[Pantheon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantheon,_Rome \"Pantheon, Rome\"),\n[Trajan's Column](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trajan's_Column \"Trajan's\nColumn\"), [Trajan's Market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trajan's_Market\n\"Trajan's Market\"), the\n[Catacombs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catacombs_of_Rome \"Catacombs of\nRome\"), the [Circus Maximus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circus_Maximus\n\"Circus Maximus\"), the [Baths of\nCaracalla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baths_of_Caracalla \"Baths of\nCaracalla\"), [Castel\nSant'Angelo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castel_Sant'Angelo \"Castel\nSant'Angelo\"), the [Mausoleum of\nAugustus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mausoleum_of_Augustus \"Mausoleum of\nAugustus\"), the [Ara Pacis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ara_Pacis \"Ara\nPacis\"), the [Arch of\nConstantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arch_of_Constantine \"Arch of\nConstantine\"), the [Pyramid of\nCestius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyramid_of_Cestius \"Pyramid of\nCestius\"), and the [Bocca della\nVerit\u00e0](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bocca_della_Verit\u00e0 \"Bocca della Verit\u00e0\")\nare renowned ancient monuments.\n\n## Romanesque\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Architecture_of_Rome&action=edit&section=2 \"Edit\nsection: Romanesque\")]\n\n[![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d1/Benfoto-\nRoma2013-087.jpg/250px-Benfoto-\nRoma2013-087.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Benfoto-\nRoma2013-087.jpg)[Cosmatesque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmatesque\n\"Cosmatesque\") pavement in the [Basilica of Saint\nPraxedes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Prassede \"Santa Prassede\")\n\nRome has a rich and diverse\n[Romanesque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanesque_architecture \"Romanesque\narchitecture\") heritage. Many of the first Christian churches in the world\nwere constructed in Rome, and Byzantine churches were mainly based on the\nRoman [basilica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica \"Basilica\"). They were\noften oblong or geometric, with three\n[naves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nave \"Nave\") and full of rich golden\nmosaics.[1] Later Romanesque churches in Rome were more round, using the Roman\narch.\n\nExamples of buildings from this period include the [Torre delle\nMilizie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torre_delle_Milizie \"Torre delle\nMilizie\"), the [Torre dei Conti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torre_dei_Conti\n\"Torre dei Conti\"), and the churches of [Santi Quattro\nCoronati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santi_Quattro_Coronati \"Santi Quattro\nCoronati\"), [Santa Prassede](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Prassede\n\"Santa Prassede\"), [Santa Maria in\nCosmedin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Maria_in_Cosmedin \"Santa Maria\nin Cosmedin\"), and [Santa Maria in\nAracoeli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Maria_in_Aracoeli \"Santa Maria\nin Aracoeli\"). Elaborate tabernacles were popular especially in the 13th\ncentury, and basilicas were often full of rich mosaics.[1] In the 12th and\n13th centuries, the [Cosmati family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmati\n\"Cosmati\") was famous for their mosaics and elegant decoration. They created\nbeautiful marble floors with mosaics, often with inlays of green and red\nporphyry. Their designs in Rome became so popular that they were known as\n[Cosmatesque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmatesque \"Cosmatesque\").[1]\n\n## Renaissance\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Architecture_of_Rome&action=edit&section=3 \"Edit\nsection: Renaissance\")]\n\nMain article: [Roman\nRenaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Renaissance \"Roman\nRenaissance\")\n\nRome is widely regarded as being the second\n[Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Renaissance \"Roman\nRenaissance\") capital of Italy after\n[Florence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence \"Florence\"), and was one of\nthe most important architectural and cultural centres of the time. It derived\nits main designs from the Classical models.\n[Loggias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loggia \"Loggia\"), or open-sided\ngalleries, were fashionable,[1] and palaces, or _palazzi_ often had\n[rusticated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rustication_\\(architecture\\)\n\"Rustication \\(architecture\\)\") blocks decorating the grand entrances to their\nhouses. In churches, especially in [St. Peter's\nBasilica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Peter's_Baldachin \"St. Peter's\nBaldachin\"), _baldacchini_ or column-supported canopies, were widely used.\nExamples include the [Palazzo del\nQuirinale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quirinal_Palace \"Quirinal Palace\")\n(now seat of the [President of the Italian\nRepublic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_Italian_Republic\n\"President of the Italian Republic\")), the [Palazzo\nVenezia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_Venezia \"Palazzo Venezia\"), the\n[Palazzo Farnese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_Farnese,_Rome \"Palazzo\nFarnese, Rome\"), the [Palazzo\nBarberini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_Barberini \"Palazzo\nBarberini\"), the [Palazzo Chigi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_Chigi\n\"Palazzo Chigi\") (now seat of the [Italian Prime\nMinister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Italy \"Prime\nMinister of Italy\")), the [Palazzo\nSpada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_Spada \"Palazzo Spada\"), the\n[Palazzo della\nCancelleria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_della_Cancelleria \"Palazzo\ndella Cancelleria\"), and the [Villa\nFarnesina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Farnesina \"Villa Farnesina\").\n\n[![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/77/0_Basilique_Saint-\nPierre_-_Rome_%282%29.JPG/250px-0_Basilique_Saint-Pierre_-\n_Rome_%282%29.JPG?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:0_Basilique_Saint-\nPierre_-_Rome_\\(2\\).JPG)The [St. Peter's\nBasilica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Peter's_Basilica \"St. Peter's\nBasilica\")\n\n## Baroque\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Architecture_of_Rome&action=edit&section=4 \"Edit\nsection: Baroque\")]\n\nFurther information: [Italian Baroque\narchitecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Baroque_architecture\n\"Italian Baroque architecture\")\n\nRome is widely regarded as being the epicentre of [Baroque\narchitecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque_architecture \"Baroque\narchitecture\"), and was profoundly influenced by the movement. [Roman baroque\narchitecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Baroque_architecture\n\"Italian Baroque architecture\") was widely based on Classical symmetry, but\nbroke many of the architectural rules, creating a far richer and more\nelaborate style, preferring grandiosity and opulence rather than Renaissance\nclassicism and elegance. [Putti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Putto \"Putto\"),\nor child cupids and cherubs, were popular in Baroque architectural design.\n\nThe city is famous for its many huge and majestic Baroque squares (often\nadorned with [obelisks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_obelisks_in_Rome\n\"List of obelisks in Rome\")), many of which were built in the 17th century.\nThe principal squares are [St. Peter's\nSquare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Peter's_Square \"St. Peter's\nSquare\"), [Piazza Navona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piazza_Navona \"Piazza\nNavona\"), [Piazza di Spagna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Steps\n\"Spanish Steps\"), [Campo de'\nFiori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campo_de'_Fiori \"Campo de' Fiori\"),\n[Piazza Venezia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piazza_Venezia \"Piazza\nVenezia\"), [Piazza\nFarnese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_Farnese,_Rome \"Palazzo Farnese,\nRome\") and [Piazza della\nMinerva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Maria_sopra_Minerva \"Santa Maria\nsopra Minerva\").\n\nOne of the most emblematic Baroque structure is the [Trevi\nFountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trevi_Fountain \"Trevi Fountain\") by\n[Nicola Salvi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicola_Salvi \"Nicola Salvi\").\nNotable 17th-century [Baroque\npalaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque_architecture \"Baroque\narchitecture\") are the [Palazzo\nMadama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_Madama,_Rome \"Palazzo Madama,\nRome\"), now the seat of the [Italian\nSenate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Senate \"Italian Senate\") and the\n[Palazzo Montecitorio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_Montecitorio\n\"Palazzo Montecitorio\"), now the seat of the [Chamber of Deputies of\nItaly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Chamber_of_Deputies \"Italian\nChamber of Deputies\").\n\n## Neoclassical Rome\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Architecture_of_Rome&action=edit&section=5 Patria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altare_della_Patria \"Altare della\nPatria\"), the best-known symbol of Roman neoclassical architecture\n\nIn 1870, Rome became the capital city of the new [Kingdom of\nItaly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Italy_\\(1861\u20131946\\) \"Kingdom\nof Italy \\(1861\u20131946\\)\"). During this time,\n[neoclassicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoclassical_architecture\n\"Neoclassical architecture\"), a building style influenced by the architecture\nof classical antiquity, became a predominant influence in Roman architecture.\nDuring this period, many great palaces in neoclassical styles were built to\nhost ministries, embassies, and other governing agencies.\n\nOne of the best-known symbols of Roman neoclassicism is the [Victor Emmanuel\nII Monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Emmanuel_II_Monument \"Victor\nEmmanuel II Monument\"), or \"Altare della Patria\", where the [Grave of the\nUnknown\nSoldier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monument_to_Vittorio_Emanuele_II#Tomb_of_Unknown_Soldier\n\"Monument to Vittorio Emanuele II\") that represents the 650,000 Italians that\nfell in [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I \"World War\nI\"), is located.\n\n## Fascist architecture\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Architecture_of_Rome&action=edit&section=6 \"Edit\nsection: Fascist architecture\")]\n\nSee also: [Fascist\narchitecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascist_architecture \"Fascist\narchitecture\")\n\n[![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/22/EUR_Piazza_Guglielmo_Marconi.jpg/250px-\nEUR_Piazza_Guglielmo_Marconi.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:EUR_Piazza_Guglielmo_Marconi.jpg)[EUR,\nRome](https://en.wikipedia"}
