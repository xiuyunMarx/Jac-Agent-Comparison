It has\nbeen ranked as the UK's top art and design educational institution.[3][4] The\nschool is organised as a department of UCL's [Faculty of Arts and\nHumanities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UCL_Faculty_of_Arts_and_Humanities\n\"UCL Faculty of Arts and Humanities\").\n\n## History\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Slade_School_of_Fine_Art&action=edit&section=1\n\"Edit section: History\")]\n\n[![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9b/SLADE_1905_B_sampled.jpeg/250px-\nSLADE_1905_B_sampled.jpeg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:SLADE_1905_B_sampled.jpeg)Students\nat the Slade in 1905\n\nThe school was founded in University College on 2 October 1871 following an\nendowment from the will of the lawyer and philanthropist [Felix\nSlade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felix_Slade \"Felix Slade\") (1788\u20131868)\nwho bequeathed \u00a335,000 to establish three Chairs in [Fine\nArt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine_Art \"Fine Art\"), to be based at Oxford\nUniversity, Cambridge University and [University College\nLondon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London \"University\nCollege London\"). An additional six scholarships of \u00a350 per annum were endowed\nto University College. The college itself provided \u00a35000 to build the Slade\nSchool as part of the college quadrangle in Gower Street, premises which the\nSchool still occupies today.[5]\n\nDistinguished past teachers include [Henry\nTonks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Tonks \"Henry Tonks\"), [Wilson\nSteer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilson_Steer \"Wilson Steer\"), [Randolph\nSchwabe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randolph_Schwabe \"Randolph Schwabe\"),\n[William Coldstream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Coldstream \"William\nColdstream\"), [Andrew Forge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Forge\n\"Andrew Forge\"), [Lucian Freud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucian_Freud\n\"Lucian Freud\"), [John\nHilliard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Hilliard_\\(artist\\) \"John\nHilliard \\(artist\\)\"), [Bruce\nMcLean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_McLean \"Bruce McLean\"), [Alfred\nGerrard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Gerrard \"Alfred Gerrard\") and\n[Phyllida Barlow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phyllida_Barlow \"Phyllida\nBarlow\"). [Edward Allington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Allington\n\"Edward Allington\") was Professor of Fine Art and Head of Graduate Sculpture\nuntil his death in 2017.[6][7]\n\nTwo of its most important periods were immediately before, and immediately\nafter, the turn of the twentieth century, described by Henry Tonks as its two\n'crises of brilliance'. The first included the students [Augustus\nJohn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustus_John \"Augustus John\"), [William\nOrpen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Orpen \"William Orpen\") and [Percy\nWyndham Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Percy_Wyndham_Lewis \"Percy\nWyndham Lewis\"); the second \u2013 which has been chronicled in [David Boyd\nHaycock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Boyd_Haycock \"David Boyd\nHaycock\")'s _A Crisis of Brilliance: Five Young British Artists and the Great\nWar_ \u2013 included the students [Dora\nCarrington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dora_Carrington \"Dora Carrington\"),\n[Mark Gertler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Gertler_\\(artist\\) \"Mark\nGertler \\(artist\\)\"), [Paul\nNash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Nash_\\(artist\\) \"Paul Nash\n\\(artist\\)\"), [C.R.W. Nevinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C.R.W._Nevinson\n\"C.R.W. Nevinson\") and [Stanley\nSpencer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanley_Spencer \"Stanley Spencer\").[8]\n\nAnother notable period followed the Second World War, under the directorship\nof [William Coldstream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Coldstream\n\"William Coldstream\"), who brought in [Lucian\nFreud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucian_Freud \"Lucian Freud\") to teach,\nand whose students included [Paula\nRego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paula_Rego \"Paula Rego\"), [Michael\nAndrews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Andrews_\\(artist\\) \"Michael\nAndrews \\(artist\\)\") and the filmmaker [Lorenza\nMazzetti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorenza_Mazzetti \"Lorenza Mazzetti\").\nColdstream was responsible for the creation of the [Slade Film\nDepartment](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/slade/sladearchive/history-of-the-slade-\nfilm-project/), the first in any British university, in 1960, with [Thorold\nDickinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thorold_Dickinson \"Thorold\nDickinson\") as chief lecturer. Filmmakers associated with the Slade Film\nDepartment include [Derek Jarman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derek_Jarman\n\"Derek Jarman\") and [Peter\nWhitehead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Whitehead_\\(filmmaker\\) \"Peter\nWhitehead \\(filmmaker\\)\").\n\n## Slade Centre for Electronic Media in Fine Art\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Slade_School_of_Fine_Art&action=edit&section=2\n\"Edit section: Slade Centre for Electronic Media in Fine Art\")]\n\nThe Slade Centre for Electronic Media in Fine Art (SCEMFA) was opened in 1995.\nThe centre provides opportunities for research into [electronic\nmedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_media \"Electronic media\") and\nfine art with the goal of contributing to debate on national and international\nlevels. The Slade had previously been home to Malcolm Hughes's Computer and\nExperimental Department in the 1970s.\n\nIn 1997 SCEMFA presented _Collision_ , a public lecture series by artists,\nwriters, and curators working with\n[interactivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interactivity \"Interactivity\"),\n[telematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telematics \"Telematics\"), and\ndigital works. This exhibition was followed by _Spontaneous Reaction_ , a\nweek-long seminar funded by the [Arts Council of\nEngland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arts_Council_of_England \"Arts Council\nof England\"), which took a critical look at interactivity with participants\nfrom a variety of disciplines, including psychology, architecture, and\n[computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science \"Computer\nscience\").\n\nThroughout 1998, SCEMFA collaborated with Channel 4 UK to organise _Cached_ ,\na monthly event held at the [Institute of Contemporary\nArts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Contemporary_Arts \"Institute\nof Contemporary Arts\"), London. Funded by the Arts Council, this series\ninvestigated the conceptual and practical issues of producing art for the\ninternet through a series of artists presentations.\n\n## Art collection\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Slade_School_of_Fine_Art&action=edit&section=3\n\"Edit section: Art collection\")]\n\nThe Slade art collection was started when the yearly prizes awarded to top\nstudents was combined with a collection scheme in 1897 and the Summer\nComposition Prize and the Figure and Head Painting Prizes began to be kept by\nthe school.[9] Works by students and staff of the Slade School of Fine Art\nform the basis of the UCL Art museum today.[9]\n\n## Rankings\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Slade_School_of_Fine_Art&action=edit&section=4\n\"Edit section:
